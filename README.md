# Acidophile pH limits submission archive

This repository is the submission snapshot for the study **Periplasmic surface charge composition predicts the lower pH limit of bacterial growth independently of growth optimum**.

It contains the analysis code, curated analysis tables, stored results, final figure files, manuscript source, tests, and reproducibility records. The 17 GB working collection of downloaded assemblies, proteomes, structures, and other re-fetchable raw inputs is intentionally excluded. Exact accessions and retrieval instructions are provided in [DATA_AVAILABILITY.md](DATA_AVAILABILITY.md).

## Submission snapshot quick start

1. Create the recorded software environment: `conda env create -f environment.yml`.
2. Activate it: `conda activate acidophile-ph-limits`.
3. Run the submission verification: `bash reproduce.sh verify`.
4. Rebuild the publication figures: `bash reproduce.sh figures`.

The verification tier uses only files committed here. Reconstructing the structural panel, HMM calls, or phylogenies requires re-fetching third-party raw inputs and is documented separately; phenotype curation involved manual reading and cannot be recreated by a fully automated command.

## Original project record

**What this is.** A comparative-genomics investigation of which genomic features set the **lowest pH at which a bacterium can grow** (`ph_min`), beyond what its growth optimum already predicts.

**What is claimed.** Acidic residues at solvent-exposed **periplasmic** positions of conserved inner-membrane proteins are associated with a lower growth limit: **+0.185 pH units per standard deviation** of a 32-protein composite with genome-wide amino-acid composition controlled (p 2.1e-10, n=2,768), falling to **+0.090** with the growth optimum also controlled. The association is specific to the periplasmic compartment — the matched cytoplasmic, transmembrane and soluble-surface controls are null or opposite — it survives within families, across 25 bootstrap trees, and in the hand-curated evidence tier, and it is the only predictor class in this project that has done so. It is also **one-sided** (confined to the acid-tolerant tail), explains **well under 1% of variance** beyond the optimum, and is not causal evidence.

**THE MODEL DOES NOT PREDICT OUT OF CLADE, THE TRANSMEMBRANE ANOMALY IS SALT, AND THE MECHANISM IS BURDEN AFTER ALL (`scripts/102`, `scripts/103`, `scripts/104`): three reachable tests, and each one came back differently from what I expected. **Held-out prediction fails.** Leave-one-phylum-out refitting asks whether a lineage carries the coefficient; it never asked whether the model transfers, because the held-out phylum still contributed to panel selection, composite standardisation and the covariates. Fitting on every other phylum with the composite re-standardised on training genomes only, pooled out-of-sample RMSE is **0.9253** pH with the composite against **0.9248** without it - fractionally worse - and the cytoplasmic control at **0.9238** does no worse than either. It lowers error in **3 of 4** folds and loses on the one that matters: Pseudomonadota, half the scored genomes and most of the acidophiles, whose removal strips the training set of the acid-tolerant tail the effect lives in. Fold gain tracks tail retained in training at **r +0.835**, the direction predicted in advance, on four points. Both readings stand: the association survives every within-sample test here and buys no out-of-sample accuracy across clades. For mechanism that is survivable; for prediction it would be fatal, and this paper claims none. **The transmembrane anomaly is salt adaptation,** and the expectation I wrote into the script's header - well-studiedness - was wrong. The signal is confined to the salt-tolerant half, **-0.21178** (p 1.4e-03) above 5% NaCl against **-0.06218** (p 3.4e-01) below; an independently-defined well-characterised subset does not reproduce it (p 0.44); covariates absorb none of it; no reference moves it more than 0.76 SE. Halotolerant membranes are compositionally distinct, exactly as the archaeal halophile result already showed - so a second environmental variable perturbs a DIFFERENT compartment from the one claimed here, which argues FOR the controls. **And the mechanism question correction 21 opened now has a measurement.** Burden and buffering want opposite signs of the same quantity, so composition alone decides: at pH 3 and 4 the charge change is **+0.13994** and **+0.07764**, acid-tolerant genomes having less outward charge to change, which is burden - while buffer capacity runs positive, the opposite of buffering. Both quantities reverse sign between pH 4 and pH 6 (**-0.20915**), where histidine titrates and its opposite-signed association shows up inside the same number. **My first version of that script was circular and said the opposite.** Evaluating each genome's quantity at its own ph_min puts the outcome inside the predictor; the cytoplasmic control, about which neither account predicts anything, fired at p 0 three lines above a verdict reading BUFFERING off the leak. That part is discarded, not reported. **Three verdicts in this session asserted the opposite of their own printed tables,** and the cause was identical each time - branches written for the outcomes I expected, with the unexpected one falling through to a default describing nothing.

**THE SALINITY CONFOUND IS CLOSED, THE SENSOR PREDICTION IS UNRUNNABLE, AND BOTH RESERVED DECISIONS ARE TAKEN (`scripts/98`, `scripts/99`, `scripts/100`, `scripts/101`): the sharpest untested objection to this project was that a 2019 paper found the same compartment ordering - secreted over cytoplasmic over membrane - for marine-versus-freshwater habitat rather than for pH. It had been recorded as blocked for want of a variable, since the habitat column is empty for all 3,002 rows. metaTraits' measured-only export turns out to carry **salinity maximum** in % NaCl for 5,199 species, 669 of which join the analysis sample. **Salinity does not predict the limit at all** (p 3.6e-01) and holding it fixed removes **1%** of the periplasmic coefficient, against a baseline refitted on the same genomes rather than the published one. The better result is in the profiles: run side by side as predictors of compartment composition, salinity gives secreted > cytoplasmic > membrane - **the 2019 ordering, reproduced here independently** - while the limit gives secreted > membrane > cytoplasmic. Salinity moves the secreted and cytoplasmic shares together as an osmotic account predicts; pH moves the secreted share and leaves the cytoplasmic one alone. The method does not impose one ordering on every variable, which is a better defence than salinity being inert - it is not inert, and predicts secreted composition MORE strongly than pH does. **It opened a question too:** on those 669 genomes the transmembrane control fires at 1.06x the periplasmic coefficient against 0.23x on the full sample, and across 300 random subsamples of equal size only **2.0%** go that far - so it is not smaller-n noise, salinity is not the cause, and what distinguishes salinity-measured genomes is unidentified. **The sensor-panel prediction was attempted and cannot be run.** Dedicated periplasmic sensor domains sit at **1.9%** detection with **100%** of detections in one phylum, so the contrast would be between lineages wearing the costume of a contrast between protein classes. The first version of that script passed its own gate on a family entered as "EvgS's periplasmic domain" - true, and misleading, because the family is the generic solute-binding fold carried at four copies per genome in 82% of bacteria. Diagnosticity is now derived from copy number and must agree with the declared flag or the script stops. **Both reserved decisions are taken.** The composite is NOT amended to include histidine despite the 25% gain, because mutual adjustment shows the two are separate mechanisms rather than one measured twice, and the charge-reversal edit leaves histidine untouched - so a histidine-bearing composite would predict an effect the experiment cannot produce and make a null uninterpretable. The two rescalings ARE adopted, taking the committed *n* to **199**, because leaving them recorded-but-unadopted keeps a design nominally feasible after it has ceased to be. But the 199-replicate experiment is **not** commissioned first: correction 21 leaves the effect's DIRECTION resting on no experiment, so the cheaper exhaustive periplasmic scan on one chassis protein - the 2001 design that worked - goes ahead of it and decides whether the larger one is worth running. **And the manuscript has figures and a release package.** Five panels, each asserting its headline values against the manuscript's own text before drawing, so a figure cannot drift from the prose it illustrates; a tiered manifest that opens by stating the pipeline does NOT re-run from the repository, because 16 GB of inputs are gitignored and the curation involved hand-reading no script replays. **One figure title had to be corrected:** the first draft said "only the periplasmic-facing surface predicts the limit", which section 3.2 of the manuscript denies in as many words - the soluble-surface control is significant and opposite in sign. A figure title is a claim and gets held to the text it illustrates.

**A CONSENSUS ACCOUNT TURNED UP THE PAPER THAT CONTRADICTS MY MECHANISM, AND IT HAD BEEN HIDDEN BY A RESULT CAP (correction 21, [`docs/EXPERIMENTAL_EVIDENCE.md`](docs/EXPERIMENTAL_EVIDENCE.md)): hours earlier I had written that no experiment existed manipulating periplasmic acidic residues in a bacterial membrane protein and measuring acid resistance. The claim was hedged as "not found by these queries" and the hedge was true and worthless, because the search returning it caps at three results per query. An exhaustive PubMed query on the same question returns **59** records, and in them is Weeks & Sachs 2001, which mutagenised **every** histidine, aspartate and glutamate in the periplasmic domain of the *H. pylori* urea channel UreI - the exhaustive periplasmic titratable-residue scan this project's whole prediction is about - and found His-123, His-131, His-193 and **Asp-129, Asp-140, Glu-138** all needed to activate transport at acidic pH, with those residues largely absent from the pH-independent *S. salivarius* homologue. **It confirms the compartment twice over and contradicts the direction.** This project has acid-tolerant genomes DEPLETED of exposed periplasmic acidic residues and offers charge burden as the reason: carboxylates protonate, outward surface charge shifts. In UreI the carboxylates are required, and the homologue lacking them cannot sense acid at all. **The two are reconcilable and the reconciliation is specific rather than a shrug:** a protein whose job is to RESPOND to acid needs titratable residues on the sensing face, while a proteome that must merely FUNCTION at low pH may do better with fewer - a dedicated gate in one channel against bulk composition over 32 housekeeping proteins. But the direct evidence now favours buffering or function over charge burden, which is the mechanism this project has been stating confidently, and the one engineering study aimed at gastric pH ADDS acidic residues on exactly that buffering argument. **The association is untouched; the explanation attached to it is weaker than it was this morning,** and that distinction is the whole content of the correction. **It also pays for itself twice.** The reconciliation makes a falsifiable prediction the project cannot currently run: a panel built from dedicated pH sensors - EvgS, CadC, UreI, none of them among the 32 - should show the OPPOSITE sign to the housekeeping panel. And the pre-registered charge-reversal design turns out to have a precedent that worked, in 2001, using the design's own method, which is worth more to it than any power calculation in it. **The lesson is about process, not about UreI.** Twenty corrections here were found by machinery built for the purpose; this one could not have been, because the machinery checks figures against stored results and nothing checks a claim about the literature. An absence claim drawn from a three-result search is not an absence claim, and I made it anyway because the search had been thorough in form.

**THE MANUSCRIPT, THE CENSORING BOUND, THE TRANSITION TEST AND THE EXPERIMENTAL LITERATURE - and the last of those found the asymmetry that matters most (`scripts/96`, `scripts/97`, [`docs/EXPERIMENTAL_EVIDENCE.md`](docs/EXPERIMENTAL_EVIDENCE.md)): a plan arrived to turn the findings into a paper rather than hunt more associations, and the first thing it revealed is that there was no manuscript to update. [`docs/MANUSCRIPT.md`](docs/MANUSCRIPT.md) now exists, organised around the three findings, with 34 signed coefficients every one of which traces to the result file its own claim cites - the best coverage of any document here - and a section listing what is still missing instead of leaving the gaps implicit. **The censoring objection is the one that could have sunk this and it is now bounded.** Most reported minima sit on a testing grid: **74%** on whole pH units, **93%** on whole or half, and the modal value pH 6.0 holds **34%** of the sample. The field that looks like it records bound type does not - the table builder assigns `measured` unconditionally to every parser row, so **97%** of the rows carrying that label were never read, and the bound, confidence and source strata that an earlier tier test reports as three separate robustness checks agree on over 95% of rows, making them one partition reported three times. The existing reassurance is about CLASSICAL error, which is independent of the predictor by construction; at equal magnitude classical noise moves the coefficient **0.006** standard errors and predictor-aligned displacement moves it **11.8**. The deliverable is the bound: zeroing the headline needs the displacement to deepen by **0.185** pH per SD of surface composition, and routed through observable proxies that is not implausible but **impossible** - the two threatening proxies need **8.36** and **16.21** pH of mean displacement against **6.04** and **6.00** pH of headroom to pH 0. Not a clearance; selection on which organisms get described at all appears in none of these proxies. **The transition test is honest rather than flattering.** Acid tolerance arises in **151** maximal wholly-tolerant clades, **33** with a usable sister; histidine shifts as predicted in 22/33 (p 0.046), the acidic measure in 19/33 (p 0.126), and the informative number is the power - 75% consistency needed against 58% observed, so underpowered rather than null. Direction holds at 5 of 5 thresholds and significance at 4, the primary not among them, which is reported in full because adopting whichever threshold cleared would be selection. **AND THE LITERATURE SEARCH INVERTED MY EXPECTATION.** Histidine - the residue whose mechanism this project understands least - has independent experimental backing from three systems, two of them periplasmic: a single periplasmic histidine is required for acid induction in EvgS, periplasmic histidines are the attributed pH gate in *H. pylori* UreI, and replacing two histidines abolishes pH-dependent activation in AdiY. The acidic arm - whose mechanism seemed obvious - has **no** directly comparable experiment, and the nearest engineering work converts residues TO acidic to survive gastric pH on a buffering rationale, the opposite sign to the charge-burden account. **The sharpest specificity threat in the project also came out of that search:** marine-to-freshwater transitions shift predicted isoelectric point with the difference sharper in secreted than cytoplasmic than membrane proteins, concentrated on surfaces - the same compartment ordering, a different variable, a different group, six years earlier. It is favourable as precedent and serious as a threat, and it is not testable here: `medium` is empty for all 3,002 rows as `oxygen` already was. The one salinity-defined subset available is archaeal, and it is the one place the compartment pattern breaks. **Four of my own errors were caught before shipping and one after:** a category error comparing SD-of-predictor against pH-per-SD, prose reading "a few hundredths of an SD" beside a table printing 0.31 to 1.41, an inverted critical-count search claiming 80% power needed 100% consistency, a specificity verdict reading "no control is significant" off a control at p 0.0506 - and two wrong figures in the manuscript's own limitations section, found by hand AFTER a clean automatic run, because the checker matches only signed coefficients and that section is written almost entirely in unsigned ones. The manuscript's status line now states that gap rather than implying coverage it does not have.

**THE ESTIMATOR REPRODUCES UNDER AN IMPLEMENTATION THAT SHARES NO CODE WITH IT (`scripts/95`): every number in this project comes from one 300-line module used by 94 scripts. Twenty corrections have been found in the analyses and **none in the estimator** - and until now the reason might simply have been that nothing had looked. A bug in `pgls` would not announce itself; it would return a plausible coefficient with a plausible standard error, and every robustness check here would inherit it, because every one of them calls it. The existing tests cannot close this: they check internal identities, and the two covariance builders both take their branch lengths from Bio.Phylo and both define covariance through the same MRCA notion, so their agreement tests the traversal rather than the definition. **Four things are independent here and they are listed so the claim is checkable rather than rhetorical:** a recursive-descent Newick parser written for this script; the covariance built from shared root-to-tip path length by longest-common-prefix comparison of ancestor chains, with no tree walk and no distance call; the estimator by symmetric eigendecomposition, explicit normal equations, a 4,001-point lambda grid and a determinant from the spectrum - no Cholesky, no `lstsq`, no Brent search; and the composite rebuilt from the panel's raw per-reference columns, floor and minimum-reference rule included, without the helper that builds it. **All 30 comparisons agree,** 10 to floating point and 20 inside tolerances declared before the run: the headline at **+0.184838** per SD against the published **+0.184837**, lambda at 0.573000 against 0.573003, the cytoplasmic (**+0.021166**) and transmembrane (**-0.043032**) controls, and both sided results to 2e-7 per point. **The first draft did not finish and the fix is the interesting part.** Eigendecomposing the covariance at each of 4,001 grid points is O(n^3) per point and does not terminate for n=2,768; the temptation at that moment is to fall back on a Cholesky sweep, which is the one route this script exists not to share. Pagel's lambda scales only the off-diagonals, so the covariance factors as a diagonal rescaling of `lambda*R + (1-lambda)*I`, which has the **same eigenvectors as R for every lambda** - one decomposition serves the whole sweep, exactly rather than approximately. **The scope is narrow and stated rather than implied:** this reproduces the fitting and the predictor's arithmetic from stored intermediates. The structural panel, the HMM detection, the exposure calls and the phenotype curation are inputs, not reproductions, and an error in any of them is invisible to this check and would be inherited by every figure it just confirmed. What it establishes is that if the headline is wrong, the fitting is not why.

**THERE IS PREDICTED `ph_min` DATA AT SCALE AND NONE OF IT CAN BE USED HERE, FOR A REASON THAT IS NOT QUALITY (`scripts/94`): the measured sample is this project's binding constraint - 137 validated rows, 3 of them high confidence - so the obvious question is whether anyone publishes *predicted* minima. They do, and every published genome-based pH predictor predicts **from one of the two things this project tests**. GenomeSPOT predicts pH from amino acid frequencies computed per predicted protein localization, extracellular proteins identified by an HMM, and reports its strongest residue-level pH correlate as the extracellular-minus-intracellular glutamate difference at **rho 0.56** - a description of this project's own measure. metaTraits' predicted `pH minimum` lists GenomeSPOT as its only model, so the whole 2.2-million-genome resource inherits it; its other models carry no pH trait, and are gene-content based, which is the *other* arm tested here. **Rather than assert the circularity from two feature lists, it is measured.** GenomeSPOT's stated feature, rebuilt in this project's data as the proteome secreted-minus-cytoplasmic D+E contrast, correlates with the panel measure at **r +0.638** and with the one-sidedness contrast at **r +0.590**, and predicts `ph_min` on its own at **+0.23432/pt** (p 1.85e-17). Both halves are needed for the verdict and the verdict is gated on both: a correlated but null feature would mean the shared part is noise, and a predictive but uncorrelated one would be independent evidence worth having. **The search returned measured data instead, which is worth more.** metaTraits ships measured-only exports whose pH sources are BacDive, BV-BRC and JGI IMG/GOLD - the latter two being the rows `docs/DATABASES.md` has listed as *not checked* since the database survey. They carry **4,323** species absent from this project, **421** at `ph_min` <= 4.0 and **51** at <= 3.0, with **767** rows reachable through neither BacDive nor anything else already used here, and an acid end that is heavily archaeal - which is the standing blocked gap. **It is a curation route and not one usable row,** and the objections are measured rather than listed: the exports are aggregated at species level where this project's unit is a strain with an assembly, which is the error that removed six *Alicyclobacillus* rows; **75%** of the new rows rest on a single observation; and bound type is as absent as it has always been, so all of them would enter at confidence `low`. The flag for implausible values is a lineage-name hint and it **under-detects** - at least two of the nine rows it fails to flag are genuine acid-mine or hot-spring isolates for which a pH 2.0 minimum is plausible - so it establishes which rows need a primary source and never which are false. **The one genuine gain is not data.** A different group, on a different label set, approaching from prediction rather than inference, arrived at the same quantity; that is external corroboration of the mechanism and the first candidate from outside this dataset that the shared-component question asks for. The caveat is stated because it is easy to overclaim: their rho is against pH *optimum*, not the minimum, and pH is their weakest output (R2 0.48, RMSE 1.1 pH), "systematically overpredicted below pH 5", which is this project's entire range. It supports the mechanism; it does not support a number. **And one figure in this entry exposed a hole in the checker:** writing coefficients into `docs/DATABASES.md` put them in a document `scripts/69` did not read, which is precisely what correction 12 was - a number free to outlive its analysis. That document is now on the checked list.

**THE HEADLINE SURVIVES A REFERENCE SET IDENTICAL FOR EVERY GENOME, BY TWO ROUTES THAT FAIL DIFFERENTLY (`scripts/93 wide`): the previous two entries created a problem for the headline that neither noticed. The measure is a share pooled across the panel, numerator *and* denominator summed over **the references a genome happens to have**. Detection runs from **54.6% to 100%**, breadth averages **28.8 of 32**, and seven references predict the limit by their presence alone - so two genomes with identical surface chemistry can get different shares because the share is pooled over different proteins. That is heterogeneity in what the measure *measures*, which holding breadth fixed in a regression does not remove, and no result in this project had ever been computed on a reference set that is the same for every genome. Two routes, run because they fail in different places: the **common core** restricts the panel to the **6** references detected in >=99% of genomes, costing references; the **full-breadth subsample** keeps all 32 and restricts to the **239** genomes carrying every one, costing sample size. **Both hold** - core net **+0.03620/pt** (p 0.012) and D+E **+0.05885/pt** (p 0.0011); subsample net **+0.08880/pt** (p 0.036) and D+E **+0.14999/pt** (p 0.0044). **And I committed correction 12 inside a script whose own preamble forbids it:** the first version headed part 2 "same genomes as above" and compared the core against the full-panel figure, when six references pool a fifth as many positions and the 200-position floor every panel result applies drops the sample from **2,768 to 1,048**. The full panel is now refitted on exactly the genomes the core retains, giving **41%** and **44%** within-sample. Two fifths is what attenuation predicts rather than a claim about the dropped references - the core pools **211** positions per genome against **1,131**, a factor of 5.4, and a noisier measure of the same quantity has its coefficient shrunk toward zero (correction 4, now used three times here). The accounts are distinguishable and the subsample decides: dropping no references and suffering none of that attenuation, it returns a **larger** per-SD effect, **+0.3540** against **+0.2341**, which attenuation predicts and a dropped-references account does not; those are different samples, so only the *direction* is claimed. What this does **not** establish is that the core is the mechanism - six references chosen for being universal are not a representative sixth of the panel, and since presence itself carries signal, a core selected on detection is selected on something related to the outcome. The claim is narrow: the measure survives being made homogeneous, by two routes that would have broken in different places.

**WHICH COMPLEXES CARRY THE BREADTH EFFECT - AND THE NEGATIVE CONTROL THE TEST WAS BUILT AROUND CANNOT RUN (`scripts/92 wide`): a count of detectable families is not a mechanism, so this asks which families. Of the **37** accepted references, **22** have enough detection variance to regress on - the other 15 are present in essentially every genome and are excluded by rule and listed, not dropped silently - and **7** survive Benjamini-Hochberg at **q<0.10**, every fit controlling composition, data source, mean alignment score, log10 genome size and assembly level. The strongest is **cytochrome bo(3) ubiquinol oxidase subunit 1** at **-0.42859** pH, with four transporters pointing the same way (YgjI **-0.19746**, EntS **-0.21519**, AroP **-0.17887**, YadH **-0.26111**) and two the other (B12 permease **+0.21893**, hydrogenase-4 component B **+0.22647**). **The intended negative control is not available, and the first draft reported it as a pass.** The five soluble references were meant to be the null - essential, universally present, predicting nothing - and they *are* universally present, at **99.1% to 100.0%** detection, which is exactly why every one falls below the variance floor and **none was tested**. "No soluble reference survives" was printed on the strength of zero having been tested; a control that cannot run is a limitation, not a pass. What replaces it is weaker and labelled so: the survivors' signs are **mixed**, five down and two up, which a shared sequencing-or-annotation artefact cannot produce because that account predicts one direction for every reference - it rules out that artefact and establishes nothing about function. Two further limits are stated rather than left implicit: detection is a crude proxy for presence, since a divergent homologue below the gathering threshold reads as absent, so these are claims about *detectable homologues of E. coli proteins* which the alignment-score control bounds but does not remove; and the concentration result sits at the edge of its own rule, breadth losing significance with the survivors held fixed while keeping **47%** against a criterion of half, so the honest form is that the named references carry about half of the breadth effect rather than being it. What it does settle is that the effect is **not uniform** across the panel - which is the signature a completeness artefact would have left, ruled out here by a second and independent route.

**THE SCORING-QUALITY NUISANCE VARIABLE IS HALF A FINDING: GENE CONTENT, NOT JUST MEASUREMENT (`scripts/91 wide`): `scripts/83 wide` found that how many panel positions a genome has scorable predicts the limit at **-2.35 pH per log10**, p 2.3e-10, and `scripts/84 wide` bounded what holding it fixed does to all 49 panel coefficients. Both treated it purely as a **control** and neither asked what it is - which is the wrong way round, because a nuisance variable with a p-value of two in ten billion is either an artefact worth naming or a finding worth having, and it had been neither for two days. Scorable positions is a product of **breadth** - how many of the 32 reference families a genome has a detectable homologue of - and **depth**, how many positions each one aligns to. Breadth is gene content, the references being respiratory and envelope complexes; depth is how well a present gene aligns to an *E. coli* reference, which is divergence and annotation. What would make it a finding was fixed before the numbers: breadth carrying it with the confounds held fixed, and depth not. **Both halves came out as their accounts predicted.** Breadth is **-0.03599 per reference (p 6.7e-04)** with assembly level, similarity to the reference organism and genome size all held fixed - 75% of its uncontrolled size - so more of these complexes detectable goes with a *lower* acid limit. Depth predicts on its own (**-1.287**, p 0.011) and carries **nothing** once similarity to *E. coli* is controlled (**+0.047**, p 0.93), which is `scripts/54`'s per-reference finding at genome level.
