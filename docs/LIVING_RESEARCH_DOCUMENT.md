One loose end from the morning kept bothering me and turned out to be worth pulling. The alignment-scorability predictor — how much of a genome's panel proteins can be classified at all — had been found by accident, measured across every panel coefficient, held fixed in the headline, and at no point had anyone asked what it actually is. That is the wrong way round for something with a p-value of two in ten billion: a nuisance variable that strong is either an artefact worth naming or a finding worth having, and for two days it had been neither. It factors cleanly. Scorable positions is breadth times depth — how many of the thirty-two reference families a genome has a detectable homologue of, and how many positions each of those homologues aligns to — and the two mean entirely different things. Breadth is gene content, since the references are respiratory and envelope complexes, so a genome missing several of them is missing those complexes. Depth is how well a gene that is present aligns to an E. coli reference, which is divergence and annotation quality and is much closer to an artefact. I wrote down before running it that the result would only be a finding if breadth carried the effect with the confounds held fixed and depth did not, and that is what happened, with both halves behaving exactly as their accounts said they would. Depth predicts the limit on its own and carries nothing once similarity to the reference organism is controlled. Breadth keeps three quarters of its coefficient with assembly level, similarity to E. coli and genome size all held fixed, so more of these complexes detectable goes with a lower acid limit. The two confounds breadth could not have escaped on its own are the ones I would have been embarrassed to have missed. The panel already stores each reference's alignment score, which is a similarity measure computed on the very sequences the breadth count comes from, so the test was available all along. Then the two gene-content scripts turned out to have created a problem for the headline that neither of them noticed, and which I only saw while writing up the second. The headline measure is a share pooled across the panel, and both its numerator and its denominator are summed over the references a genome happens to have. Detection runs from about half to all, breadth averages twenty-nine of thirty-two, and seven references predict the limit by their presence alone — so two genomes with identical surface chemistry can come out with different shares simply because the share was pooled over different proteins. That is not a confound to be controlled; it is heterogeneity in what the measure measures, and holding breadth fixed in a regression does nothing about it. Nothing in this project had ever been computed on a reference set that is the same for every genome. There are two ways to fix it and they fail in different places, which is the reason to run both. Restricting the panel to the references detected in essentially every genome makes each genome's share come from an identical set and costs references — only six survive that threshold. Restricting to the genomes that carry the whole panel keeps all thirty-two and costs sample size, leaving two hundred and thirty-nine. Both hold, with the same sign and comfortable significance, so the headline is not an artefact of pooling over whichever proteins a genome happens to have. The last thing attempted was a confound that had been sitting in plain sight for weeks, hidden by the fact that the project had always discussed it as a property of the wrong objects. Every per-genome number in this work is produced by aligning that genome's protein to one of thirty-two Escherichia coli reference structures and reading off residues at the reference's exposed periplasmic positions, and a genome is scored on a reference only when enough of those positions align. Coverage has therefore been talked about constantly — but always as a property of the references, as the question of which proteins are prevalent enough across species to include in the panel. As a property of the genomes it is something else entirely, because how many of the thirty-two a given organism can be scored on is a measure of how divergent it is from E. coli, acidophiles are phylogenetically clustered, and so divergence from E. coli and acid tolerance would be expected to travel together whether or not any of the biology is real. The reflex answer is that the phylogenetic regression handles it, and that reflex is wrong: a lambda-scaled Brownian covariance absorbs variance shared among relatives, but distance to one particular reference organism is a specific direction on the tree rather than autocorrelation among the tips, and nothing in the model knows anything about it. A pure property of the alignment pipeline predicts the growth limit about as strongly as the biological claim does. What it does to the coefficient, though, is almost nothing, and the reason is the second leg a confound needs and does not have here: scorability barely moves the predictor, correlating only plus 0.086 with the composite and coverage minus 0.112, so controlling both leaves the headline at 0.180 against 0.185. Among the two hundred and seventy genomes that score on twenty-eight or more references, where scorability hardly varies at all, the estimate is larger rather than smaller. The test that actually settles it is specificity, and it settles it cleanly, because an artefact of the alignment scores every position class through the very same alignments against the very same references and therefore cannot produce a coefficient in the periplasmic class while leaving the matched cytoplasmic one null. Controlling scorability and coverage moves every one of the five classes by at most six thousandths of a standard deviation, which is to say it moves none of them. **2026-10-27.** Two things today, one a check on whether the central p-value should be believed at all and one a change in what the association is understood to be. The check first (script 50). Every p-value in the project comes from phylogenetic generalised least squares on a neighbour-joining tree with Pagel's lambda fitted by maximum likelihood, and two features of that setup could make the p-values too small without anyone noticing: the predictor is itself strongly autocorrelated on the tree, since close relatives have similar surface composition, and the tree is a distance tree whose errors a bootstrap of its own alignment cannot detect. A parametric bootstrap addresses both. The response was simulated with no effect of the predictor at all, drawn from the null model's own multivariate normal with mean and covariance taken from the fitted null, while the predictor kept its real values and therefore its real phylogenetic structure, and then the project's own test was run on the simulated response. Five hundred draws at each of three generating lambdas: the fitted value, where the model is correct; one, pure Brownian motion, which is the realistic misspecification because lambda is fitted on the covariates-only model; and zero, the opposite error. Testing always at the fitted lambda, held fixed, which is a concession made for cost and which removes a source of null variability, so the rates are if anything optimistic about the test. With the model correct the nominal five percent test rejects 6.2 percent of the time and the nominal one-in-a-thousand test never rejects in 500 draws; under Brownian misspecification the rates are 7.2 percent and 0.2 percent. So there is mild inflation, around 1.2 to 1.4 times, and nothing like the orders of magnitude that would be needed to explain the observed result: the real statistic is 6.22 and the largest value in all 1,500 null draws is 3.61, so the finding lies beyond the entire simulated null. The more interesting consequence concerns the soluble-surface control, which has been awkward throughout. Its real coefficient is -0.094 with a nominal p of 0.015, which had to be reported as a weak effect in the opposite direction, a claim with no mechanistic account behind it. Under Brownian misspecification the nominal five percent test rejects 13 percent of the time for that predictor and the null statistic reaches 4.99, so the observed 2.44 is unremarkable. The right reading is that the soluble control is null, not oppositely signed, and the specificity argument is cleaner for it rather than weaker. Second, and more substantive, the relationship is not linear and the project has been describing it wrongly by quoting a single slope (script 51). The test had to be built carefully, because binning on the outcome restricts its range inside each bin and attenuates the slope by construction; the predictor was binned instead and the mean limit estimated per quintile. So a deficit of acidic residues on exposed periplasmic surfaces marks acid tolerance, and an excess above average carries no information about the limit. Then went back and tested an explanation I had published as though it were established (script 52). Correction 4 in the synthesis accounts for the rule-selected panel of 32 references giving a larger standardised effect than the hand-picked 7 by saying the composite is a noisy measure of one latent quantity, so averaging more references removes measurement error and raises the coefficient towards the latent effect. Classical measurement error attenuates a slope by exactly the reliability of the predictor, so that explanation makes a sharp prediction: the coefficient divided by the reliability should be the same number at every panel size. The fair summary is that the measurement-error account is supported where there is enough panel width to test it and unsupported where there is not, that it remains the best available explanation of the panel-size effect, and that it is no longer merely asserted. That last conclusion had to be revised within the hour, because the disattenuation itself was wrong. Classical measurement error attenuates a slope expressed per unit of the predictor by the reliability, but these coefficients are per standard deviation of the observed predictor, and that standard deviation is itself inflated by the noise, so the per-standard-deviation coefficient attenuates by the square root of the reliability and must be divided by the square root to correct it. **2026-10-22.** Started the acidophile project by adapting the halophile plan to the lower pH limit. Built the phenotype schema with bound-type handling (censored "lowest tested" values are kept out of the primary set), weak-acid chemistry helpers, proteome and genome feature extraction (acidic excess with sign unassumed, predicted pI, hydrophobicity, Cys and His, GC, dinucleotides, IVYWREL as a thermal control), a 28-system catalog across nine acid-resistance categories with provisional annotation-keyword counting, the six-model comparison under family-blocked and leave-one-lineage-out validation with group-bootstrap confidence intervals, and matched close-relative contrasts. Thirteen tests pass, including checks that no lineage straddles a train/test split, that a planted gene-content signal is recovered, and that a null dataset is not reported as a success; these use synthetic data only. No real strains, genomes or results exist yet, and no biological claim is made. Began data acquisition from BacDive's public API for 31 candidate genera (acidophilic iron/sulfur oxidisers, acidophilic heterotrophs, thermoacidophilic archaea, and acid-tolerant contrasts), caching 600 or so raw records. Only 46 strains carry any pH information, 30 have a lower pH bound, 17 have minimum, optimum, maximum and an NCBI assembly together, and only 2 are archaea. BacDive gives growth ranges without saying whether growth failed beyond them, so every bound was recorded as unknown type and low confidence, which means none qualifies for the primary set yet. Two parser faults were found and fixed during this step: hyphenated ranges were read as negative numbers, and single growth-pH values were read as zero-width ranges. Assembly accessions arrive without version numbers and still need resolving. No genomes have been downloaded and no models have been run on real data. Continued with literature curation: 138 Europe PMC species-description abstracts containing a pH range were extracted by regex, then every row was read against its quoted statement. 97 were curated by hand (bound type recorded as measured only where the wording describes a growth range, and as censored where it describes tested points), 40 were excluded with a stated reason (optimum-only statements, isolation-site pH, co-cultures, neutrophiles with minimum above 5, and papers where several species share one pH sentence). Assemblies were resolved through NCBI Datasets with a rule that the species epithet must match, which rejected synonym substitutions that would have paired a phenotype with a different strain; 14 unannotated GenBank assemblies were replaced by their RefSeq copies. The resulting table has 108 strains with a matched assembly and a primary set of 66 across 31 families and 17 classes (50 bacteria, 16 archaea), with pH_min from 0 to 6. All 66 genomes and proteomes were downloaded and checksum-verified. First real run: the optimum-only model explains most of the variance in pH_min (R-squared about 0.78 under family-blocked validation), and no proteome, gene-content, pathway or combined model beat it on held-out families or held-out classes; models without the optimum performed far worse. The arginine-deiminase detector was found present in every genome because its keywords matched ordinary arginine biosynthesis enzymes, and was narrowed to arginine deiminase alone. Matched close-relative contrasts found only 3 within-genus and 7 within-family taxa, below the threshold for a test. Limitations: literature bounds are medium confidence at best because abstracts rarely state that growth failed beyond the range; the temperature of pH measurement is missing for every strain; system detection is keyword-based on product names and unvalidated; the sample is small and dominated by a few families. Next-step round: the HMM validation was done by building a 24-system Pfam panel (every accession checked against its name and description before use) and comparing it with the keyword calls across the 66 and then 89 genomes; keyword recall was very low for Kef (3 percent), Cfa (25 percent), Gad and the iron-oxidation cupredoxin, and precision was poor for arsenic resistance and the S-layer, so all later models use the HMM features, with family-level caveats recorded for Gad, Adi/Cad, Complex I, cupredoxins and copper oxidases, and six systems (GDGT ring synthase, sulfur oxidation, exopolysaccharide, organic-acid catabolism and two others) remain unvalidated for lack of a clean model. Literature expansion: a plain-term Europe PMC sweep by year returned 15,259 abstracts, of which the strict extractor kept 69 new species descriptions with an acid-relevant pH range; open full text was retrievable for 246 of 473 acid-related descriptions and added about a dozen species plus explicit methods evidence. Bound verification: a minimum is now marked verified (confidence high) only when the tested pH interval in the methods extended below the reported minimum or the text states no growth below it, which applies to 7 strains, and only 3 of those have a type-material assembly; every other literature bound stays medium or low.
