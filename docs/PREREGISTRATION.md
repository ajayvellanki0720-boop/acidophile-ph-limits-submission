# Pre-registration: charge reversal at exposed periplasmic positions

**Status: written 2026-10-29, before any strain has been built or any data collected.** Nothing in this document may be changed once the first variant is constructed except through the deviations procedure in Section 10, which requires the change and its reason to be recorded with a date.

This fixes the analysis. `docs/CHARGE_REVERSAL_DESIGN.md` fixes the construction and is the companion document; where the two disagree about an analysis choice, this one governs. Every number here is computed in `scripts/70_experiment_power.py` or taken from `docs/NUMBERS.md`, which is authoritative over prose.

## Why this document exists

`docs/SYNTHESIS.md` Section 5 lists **twenty-one** corrections, twenty of them to this project's own analyses and one to a literature-evidence claim (21). They are worth reading before trusting anything below, because they are not scattered mistakes — they share a structure. A per-unit coefficient compared against a per-standard-deviation one (corrections 4 and 6). A disattenuation by reliability where the arithmetic called for its square root (7). A model that silently dropped a covariate (9). A proxy two-thirds false positives used to bound an error that was directly detectable (10). A change-point location quoted to three decimals from a profile interval when resampling put it anywhere across a third of the range (11). A decomposition computed across two different samples (12). A per-standard-deviation coefficient stored under a per-unit name — the same confusion as 4 and 6, running the other way (15). A claim asserted flatly from four references that holds only conditionally on thirty-two (16). And a pipeline whose output depended on the process rather than on its inputs, in four scripts rather than the one first reported (17). And a refusal that was correct about the floor and wrong about what the floor meant (18). And a residue share entered into a *control* without the correlate it shares variance with — corrections 14 and 19 are the same error, and 19 is the first time this project made it somewhere other than in a headline. And the same error again in the headline *measure*: the exposed acidic share is inflated about 27% by omitting exposed histidine, which the panel has only carried since 31 October (20). And one that is not an analysis error at all: an absence claim about the experimental literature drawn from a search capped at three results per query, where an exhaustive query returns fifty-nine records and the most relevant experiment in the project (21) — the only correction here that no part of this project's checking apparatus could have caught, because it checks figures and not literature.

Every one of those was found **after** publishing the figure, and all but one were found by deriving the quantity a second way rather than by noticing two numbers disagree. An experiment designed by that same process and analysed with the same freedom would inherit the same failure mode, with the difference that bench work cannot be re-run cheaply. So the analysis is committed here, in advance, including the parts that are unfavourable.

## 1. Hypothesis and direction

Acidic residues at solvent-exposed **periplasmic** positions of conserved inner-membrane proteins contribute causally to the lowest pH at which a bacterium grows. Specifically: replacing aspartate and glutamate with lysine at such positions **lowers** pH_min, making the strain more acid-tolerant; the reverse substitution raises it.

This is a directional prediction. A shift in the opposite direction at conventional significance refutes it and will be reported as a refutation, not as an unexpected finding requiring reinterpretation.

## 2. Primary endpoint — one test

**Endpoint.** ΔpH_min between the NuoL + NuoN combined variant (14 positions reversed) and the reconstructed wild type, measured in the confirmation assay: pH_min read from a continuous fitted pH–growth-rate curve, not a visual threshold.

**Test.** Two-sided two-sample *t*-test on pH_min, α = 0.05, no correction, because this is the single pre-specified primary test. Genotype blinded during curve fitting and scoring; plate positions randomised.

**Everything else in the experiment is secondary** — the nine-point dose series, the six controls, the reverse YidC arm, both acid conditions, and the pooled competition assay. Secondary results are reported with Bonferroni correction across the **17** declared comparisons (1 primary, 9 dose, 6 controls, 1 reverse), i.e. α = 0.0029, and a secondary result that clears only the uncorrected threshold will be described as such and not as a finding.

This designation is the point. `scripts/68` found that multiplicity correction would *not* have caught this project's most serious false lead — the Kdp ATPase reached p 1.2e-08 and was still wrong — so correction is not being treated as a guarantee of anything. It is simply the difference between one pre-specified test and seventeen chances, and the pre-specification is what matters.

## 3. Sample size, and an unfavourable consequence of computing it honestly

The design document powers the confirmation assay for a **−0.16** pH-unit effect, which is the prediction obtained by scaling through the 7-reference hand-picked panel. The same calculation on the 32-reference rule-selected panel gives **−0.05**. The gap is threefold, the comparative data do not choose between the two, and `docs/NUMBERS.md` records that the two panels are different measurements whose per-unit figures are not comparable.

Powering for the larger and observing nothing yields an uninterpretable null, which is the worst available outcome. **This pre-registration therefore powers for the conservative −0.05.** At the design's stated assay SD of 0.1 (`scripts/70`):

| Predicted effect | α basis | n per group |
|---|---|---|
| −0.05 (32-reference, conservative) | 0.05, primary test | **64** |
| −0.05 | 0.05/17, secondary arms | 119 |
| −0.16 (7-reference, the design's figure) | 0.05, primary test | 8 |
| −0.16 | 0.05/17 | 14 |

The design's floor of n ≥ 20 detects **0.091** pH units at α 0.05 and 0.128 under correction — so against the conservative prediction it is underpowered by a factor of **1.8**, and the floor was set against the larger effect. The committed requirement is **n ≥ 64 per group for the primary endpoint**, and arms that cannot be run at that depth are declared underpowered in advance rather than analysed and explained afterwards.

**The assay SD is the parameter that decides feasibility and it has no source.** The design states 0.1 without citation. At SD 0.05 the conservative case needs n = 17; at SD 0.2 it needs n = 253. Measuring it is Gate 1 below.

## 4. Gates that must pass before the panel is built

Each gate has a pre-specified outcome that stops or reshapes the experiment. A gate that fails and is then waived must be recorded under Section 10.

**Gate 1 — assay SD.** Measure the SD of fitted pH_min across ≥ 20 independent replicates of the reconstructed wild type. If SD > 0.15, the conservative case needs n ≥ 143 and the confirmation assay as designed is not viable; the experiment is re-scoped rather than run.

**Gate 2 — the pooled assay's units.** The pooled competition readout is log relative fitness, not pH_min, and converting between them needs a slope — fitness per pH unit of growth limit — that has not been measured for this chassis or any other. `scripts/70` declines to invent one. Required pilot: compete the reconstructed wild type against a ladder of strains whose pH_min differs by independently measured amounts, across the same pH series, and regress log fitness on known pH_min difference. **Until that slope exists with a standard error, the design's claim to resolve 0.02 pH units is an assumption and the dose series is not interpreted.**

**Gate 3 — the interpretability gate**, as the design document specifies: any variant with reduced abundance, mislocalisation, or reduced function at pH 7 is excluded from the primary analysis and reported separately. Pre-specified because a destabilised protein otherwise masquerades as an acid-tolerance effect. Exclusion thresholds are to be fixed in writing before unblinding, and are part of Gate 1's pilot.

## 5. Decision rule, written before the data

| Observation | Reading |
|---|---|
| Primary variant shifts ≤ −0.05, p < 0.05; cytoplasmic and buried-periplasmic controls null; same-charge control null | **Supported.** The strongest available outcome |
| Primary shifts as predicted, but the matched cytoplasmic control shifts too | **Not supported.** Indicates a general membrane-protein or construction effect, which is what that control exists to detect |
| Primary shifts as predicted; same-charge (D↔E) control also shifts | **Not supported.** Any mutation at these sites does it |
| Primary null, Gate 1 and Gate 2 passed, n ≥ 64 | **Evidence against**, bounded as in Section 6 |
| Primary null, n < 64 or a gate failed | **Uninformative.** Will be reported as uninformative, not as evidence against |
| Primary shifts in the opposite direction, p < 0.05 | **Refuted** |
| Dose series monotonic over the range where Gate 2 established sensitivity | Supportive, secondary |
| Dose series non-monotonic | Not evidence against the primary result; the lower half of the series is below the detection floor by construction |

## 6. What a null would and would not establish — declared in advance

This is the clause most at risk of being written after the fact, so it is written now.

`scripts/55` measured how well structural annotation transfers between reference structures of the same Pfam family: per-genome measurements scored against different references of one family correlate only **+0.252** (NuoL vs NuoN), +0.079 and +0.087 (each against HyfB), and +0.126 (SecY *E. coli* vs *B. subtilis*) — median **+0.126**. The pipeline is not at fault; the same accession scored through both panels returns r = 1.000. So a per-genome exposed-periplasmic value is largely specific to the reference it was scored against.

It follows that **a null in NuoL and NuoN bounds the effect of reversing those 14 positions in those two proteins in *E. coli*, and very little else.** It does not bound the compartment-level hypothesis, because the comparative result is an aggregate over 32 references and no single protein's estimate should be read as biology (`scripts/54`: a reference's own coefficient correlates **+0.817** with how cleanly it can be scored). This asymmetry is uncomfortable and is stated here rather than discovered later: a positive result is strong evidence, a null is weak evidence, and that is a property of the design, not a defence constructed after seeing a null.

The mitigation is a second forward arm in a different Pfam family. **The arm is P24177** (probable aminoglycoside efflux pump, PF00873): 39 positions eligible under `scripts/37b`'s criterion against 27 for YidC, NuoL and NuoN combined, 21 of them positive, and the panel's highest reliability at 0.449 (`scripts/71`) — though that last point is **not** independent evidence, since a reference of its size is expected to score 0.479 from measurement averaging alone (`scripts/72`); the argument is its acidic density, 2nd of 32 and independent of size. Expect the interpretability gate to reject a larger fraction of its variants than of the primary arm's, because its eligible positions sit in a folded porter domain rather than short loops. If capacity permits one addition, it is that one.

This document originally named **AmtB** here and that was withdrawn within the day as correction 13: AmtB has 2 eligible positions, both of the wrong sign. The recommendation had been made on panel-level merit without checking editability, which is a free filter. It is recorded rather than quietly edited because the same failure — reaching for the available number instead of the relevant one — is what most of Section 5's corrections are.

## 7. What will not count as evidence

Each of these is a move this project has actually made and had to withdraw.

- Re-scaling the predicted effect after seeing the observed one, in either direction. `scripts/63` found the local slope at the *E. coli* composite is 1.55× the panel average, which would raise the predictions to −0.078 and −0.248; that adjustment was **declined** and is declined here too, because the hinge was fitted on the data that suggested the one-sidedness and the local slope is a selected quantity.
- Switching between the 7-reference and 32-reference scaling after the fact. The commitment is the conservative figure, fixed in Section 3.
- Selecting a subset of variants, replicates or pH values on the outcome. Exclusions come only from the pre-specified gates.
- Quoting a per-unit effect against a per-standard-deviation prediction, or any comparison of figures computed on different samples. See corrections 4, 6 and 12.
- Treating a secondary arm that clears only the uncorrected threshold as a finding.

## 8. Reporting commitment

All arms are reported whether or not they support the hypothesis, including variants excluded by Gate 3 and arms declared underpowered. Barcode counts, fitted curves and the blinding log are published with the analysis. If the experiment is abandoned at a gate, the gate result is published.

## 9. Standing uncertainties this experiment does not address

Recorded so that a positive result is not over-read.

- **The association is one-sided** and lives in the acid-tolerant tail. *E. coli* sits at −0.274 SD, the 37th percentile, below the change point's entire profile interval, so the chassis is in the live half of the range (`scripts/63`, `scripts/64`) — but the experiment tests one organism at one position in that range.
- **43% of the comparative association is independent of the growth optimum**, 57% runs through it (`scripts/57`). The experiment measures pH_min and does not separate the two.
- **The comparative claim needs only UniProt topology, not AlphaFold** (`scripts/59`: periplasmic-all gives +0.1871 against the exposure-filtered +0.1848), so the specificity that matters is periplasmic against cytoplasmic. The buried-periplasmic control is therefore a folding control first and a specificity control second.
- **Only 5 species have a demonstrated limit** in the strict sense, and `scripts/65` closed the cheap route to improving that. The comparative outcome variable is mostly a lowest-pH-tested, not a shown boundary.
- **Half the rationale for reversing rather than neutralising does not replicate** (`scripts/36c wide`, `scripts/74`; synthesis corrections 14 and 15). The substitution is D/E → K, which is worth its extra cost over removing the acid alone only if the basic residue contributes independently. On the 7-reference panel lysine pooled at −0.0136 per percentage point (p 0.0056) against D+E at +0.0083, so the basic half was larger than the acidic half; on the 32-reference panel that carries the headline the acidic half is intact (+0.0086, p 3.4e-10) and lysine falls to **−0.0024 at p 0.10 — not significant on that panel at all**, consistent in 11 of 32 references. As a ratio in which the per-position scaling cancels, reversal's margin over neutralisation falls from **2.62× to 1.27×**. This does **not** change the committed predicted effect in Section 3, which rests on the acidic term and is unchanged, and it is not an argument for switching to an uncharged substitution — that trade depends on constructability and interpretability, which this analysis cannot weigh. It is recorded because the margin was quoted from the narrow panel.
- **The acidic signal is carried mostly by glutamate** (`scripts/36c wide`). On 32 references E pools at +0.038 per SD of its share and is positive in 31 of 32, while D pools at +0.010 and is positive in 18 of 32. Per percentage point (`scripts/74`) the two are +0.0106 and +0.0026.
- **Entered simultaneously the two residues are separable, and the arm is worth fewer than its 14 positions** (`scripts/75 wide`). Fitting D and E together, so each coefficient holds the other fixed, is legitimate here because the two shares are nearly independent across genomes (median r −0.143, max VIF 1.16). Per percentage point, aspartate pools at +0.0050 (23 of 32) and glutamate at +0.0119 (32 of 32), a real difference (p 0.002) that survives controlling GC and genome size at +0.0061 (p 0.004) — the one confound specific to comparing two acidic residues, since their codons differ at the third position's GC. Glutamate supplies **81%** of the arm's expected effect and the 5 aspartate positions **19%**, so the arm is worth **11.1 glutamate-equivalent positions** rather than 14, a 21% shortfall. On the hand-picked panel (`scripts/75`) the two are not distinguishable at all and the shortfall is 9%.
- **The eligibility criterion cannot express that difference** (`scripts/37b`). It selects any position where the reference carries D *or* E, so it does not distinguish the stronger component from the weaker. The primary arm is **9 glutamate and 5 aspartate** — the favourable direction, but by accident of ranking rather than by design, since 37b ranks on each position's own association and never on its residue. If the arm is ever re-selected, glutamate positions should be preferred.

  **What this would do to Section 3, computed but NOT adopted.** If a position's contribution is proportional to its own residue's coefficient — an assumption that nothing has tested — the committed −0.05 becomes −0.0397 and the required *n* rises from **64 to 101** per group (`scripts/75 wide`). This is recorded rather than applied: Section 3's commitment stands, and changing it is a decision for the deviations procedure in Section 10, taken deliberately rather than discovered at the bench. The assumption is the weak link, not the arithmetic.

  **And it is no longer the only unadopted rescaling of that number** (2026-11-01, synthesis correction 20, `scripts/88 wide`). A second one adjusts the same −0.05 for a different reason — which residues the model holds fixed rather than which positions contribute: the exposed acidic coefficient is inflated about 28.5% by omitting exposed histidine, and the experiment should inherit the controlled figure because the edit it performs leaves histidine untouched. On its own that gives **−0.036** and **n ≥ 126**. **The two had never been multiplied.** Their product is **0.5680**, which takes the design target to **−0.0284**, the margin over the 0.02 resolution target to **1.4×**, and the replicate requirement to **n ≥ 199**. That rests on an independence assumption nothing has tested — the two adjustments are plausibly multiplicative, and if they interact the product is wrong. Neither is adopted and Section 3 stands. The reason for computing the product is that two unadopted reductions recorded in two separate documents is precisely how a design stays nominally feasible while actually ceasing to be.

## 9a. The two reserved decisions, taken 2026-11-01

Two questions had been deliberately left to this procedure rather than to a script. Both are now decided, with reasons, so that neither sits in the unstable state of being computed, recorded and unadopted.

### Decision 1 — the composite is NOT amended. `net D+E−K−R` stands.

`scripts/87 wide` computed what adding histidine would do: `net D+E−K−R−H` gives **+0.2924 per SD** against **+0.2342**, a 25% increase, and that figure has sat in the synthesis as computed-not-adopted since correction 20.

**Rejected, and not on grounds of conservatism.** A composite asserts that its terms measure one thing. `scripts/87 wide` establishes that they do not: under mutual adjustment the acidic share keeps 73% and histidine 77%, both at p<1e−8, which is the signature of two mechanisms that co-vary rather than one measured twice. Correction 21 sharpens this — histidine's proton-sensing mechanism is independently supported in four bacterial systems, and the acidic arm's charge-burden account is the one the direct evidence does *not* favour. They are different mechanisms with different standing.

**The decisive point is specific to this experiment.** The charge-reversal edit alters acidic residues and leaves histidine untouched — that is exactly why `scripts/88 wide` has the design inheriting the histidine-controlled figure. A composite including histidine would therefore predict an effect size the experiment is structurally incapable of producing, and a null would be uninterpretable: it could mean the acidic mechanism is absent, or merely that two thirds of the predicted effect was never on the table. The 25% is real signal belonging to a mechanism this design does not manipulate.

### Decision 2 — n ≥ 199 is not commissioned. The experimental programme is reordered instead.

Section 3 commits to *n* ≥ 64 per group. Section 9 records an unadopted rescaling giving 101, correction 20 a second giving 126, and `scripts/88 wide` their product: target **−0.0284**, margin over the assay's 0.02 resolution **1.4×**, and ***n* ≥ 199**.

**The two rescalings are adopted as the honest estimate.** Leaving them recorded-but-unadopted is the state Section 9 itself warns about: the design stays nominally feasible at 64 while actually requiring 199. Their product rests on an untested independence assumption, which is stated, but the alternative — using a number known to be optimistic — is worse than using one known to be approximate.

**The 199-replicate experiment is nonetheless not the next thing to build,** and correction 21 is why. That design tests the magnitude of an effect whose *direction* now rests on no experiment at all: the single exhaustive mutagenesis of periplasmic titratable residues in a bacterial membrane protein found carboxylates **required** for low-pH function, the opposite of this design's charge-burden rationale. Committing 199 replicates per group to measure the size of an effect whose sign is in question is the wrong order of operations.

**What is commissioned instead** is the cheaper discriminating experiment that correction 21 also supplies the template for: an exhaustive periplasmic titratable-residue scan on a single chassis protein with a pH-dependent functional readout — the Weeks & Sachs 2001 design, which worked. It separates charge burden from buffering, needs no power calculation because it reads out per residue rather than per group, and its result determines whether the 199-replicate design is worth running at all. If carboxylate removal helps at low pH, the charge-reversal design proceeds at *n* ≥ 199. If it hurts, the design is testing a mechanism that does not exist and the comparative association needs a different explanation before any strain is built.

**Nothing in Sections 1–8 changes.** The hypothesis, endpoint, decision rule and reporting commitment stand as written. What changes is the *order*, and the recorded sample size, both entered in Section 10 below.
