# Genomic determinants of the lower growth-pH limit: synthesis

State of the project as of 2026-10-27. The chronological record is `docs/LIVING_RESEARCH_DOCUMENT.md`; this document states what is claimed, on what evidence, what was withdrawn, and what would change the conclusion.

---

## 1. The claim

**In bacteria, acidic residues on solvent-exposed, periplasmic-facing positions of conserved membrane proteins are associated with a *higher* lower growth-pH limit.** Acid-tolerant bacteria have fewer acidic residues on those specific surfaces. The relationship is carried by glutamate and by the absence of lysine, is distributed across many surface positions rather than a few sites *wherever a reference has a signal of its own* (correction 16 — the unconditional form does not survive the 32-reference panel), and is not explained by genome-wide amino-acid composition, GC content, genome size, or the growth optimum.

**The association is one-sided** (`scripts/51`). Binned on the predictor rather than on the outcome, the effect is confined to the two lowest quintiles of the composite — Q1 **−0.389** and Q2 **−0.239** pH against the middle quintile — and is flat above it (Q4 −0.016, p 0.76; Q5 +0.070, p 0.23), with a negative squared term (−0.036, p 0.009). A *deficit* of acidic residues on exposed periplasmic surfaces marks acid tolerance; an excess above average carries no information about the limit. That is the asymmetry the proposed mechanism implies, and it is the half of the range the experiment operates in. **Put precisely** (`scripts/63`, tested in `scripts/64`): a hinge model fitted by profile likelihood beats both the line and the quadratic on AIC (6973.2 against 6982.0 and 6977.2), with a slope of roughly **+0.29 per SD below the break** and **+0.04 above** it, the change being −0.246 ± 0.069. **The break's location is not estimable to any useful precision and should not be quoted as a number.** The point estimate is +0.217 SD with a 95% profile interval of [−0.147, +0.548], but across 200 random half-samples of the 428 families the fitted break ranges from −1.32 to +1.22 with a 10th-to-90th-percentile span of +0.11 to +1.22 — so the profile interval understates the uncertainty badly. The hinge is also preferred in only 5 of 6 specifications, and **not** once the growth optimum is controlled (ΔAIC +2.54), so part of it may be the optimum's own nonlinearity. What is robust is the ordinal claim the experimental design needs: *E. coli*, the chassis, sits at −0.274 SD (37th percentile) and falls **below the fitted break in all six specifications, below its entire profile interval in three of them, and in 198 of 200 half-samples (99.0%)**. It is in the live part of the range, and the forward arm moves it further in. The local slope is 1.55× the average, but the design's predicted effects are deliberately **not** revised upward on that: the hinge was fitted on the data that suggested the one-sidedness, so the local slope is a selected quantity; 1.55× is small beside the unresolved threefold gap between the 32- and 7-reference scalings; and raising a predicted effect is the direction that flatters the experiment. Caveat on the flat top: reported minima pile up near pH 5–6 for ordinary neutrophiles, so some of it may be the phenotype's ceiling rather than biology — `scripts/62` rules out random error as the cause but not a shared reporting convention.

**Effect size: about +0.18 pH units per standard deviation** of the exposed-periplasmic composite with genome-wide composition controlled, falling to **+0.090** with the growth optimum also controlled (p 0.0036, n=1,655). Those two figures are on different samples, because the optimum is known for only 1,655 of 2,768 genomes, so the gap between them is **not** the optimum's share; on the optimum-known sample alone the composition-only estimate is +0.2123, and the decomposition is 57% through the optimum against 43% independent of it (`scripts/57`, correction 12). **A further control for exposed histidine, which correction 20 showed the acidic measure had been borrowing from, leaves 39% rather than 43%** (`scripts/89 wide`): on the pooled share the measure goes from +0.09045 per point with composition controlled to **+0.03502** with the optimum and histidine both held fixed, **+0.1120** per SD at p 0.0039. The two controls are close to independent of each other — the product of their separate shares is 38% against 39% jointly — so their costs compound rather than overlap. This is the hardest test the claim currently has and it passes: the share quoted is scale-free because the absolute figures come from the pooled share rather than the factor-score composite. The figure of +0.11 quoted here previously came from a model that controlled the optimum but silently dropped the composition term; see correction 9 and `docs/NUMBERS.md`, which states every headline figure with its panel, sample, model and both scales. (These replace +0.07 and +0.042, reported earlier from a script that returned the slope per unit of the composite rather than per standard deviation; see correction 6.) This is small. For comparison, the growth optimum alone explains most of the variance in pH_min (R² 0.82 in the earlier full-dataset models), and no genomic model has ever beaten it.

**How much is it worth as a predictor? Almost nothing.** Out of sample, with ten-fold cross-validation blocked by family (whole families held out, so family means cannot be memorised), on the 1,657 bacteria with all three variables: the growth optimum alone gives R² **0.578**, the composite alone **0.178**, and the two together **0.584**. Adding the composite to the optimum is worth **+0.0065 of R²** — six tenths of a percentage point of variance — though it improves the prediction in 20 of 20 random fold assignments, so the gain is consistent rather than noise. Genome-wide acidic excess adds nothing at all (0.5776 against 0.5776).

Both halves of that are true and belong together: the association is robust, specific, replicated across every major lineage and present within genera, **and** it improves prediction of the lower limit by well under one percent of variance beyond the growth optimum. (The project's earlier figure of R² 0.82 for the optimum was in-sample on 356 species; 0.578 is out-of-sample and family-blocked on 1,657.)

This is an **association from comparative data**. It is not established as causal; only the experiment addresses that.

## 2. Dataset

2,771 bacteria and 327 archaea with a growth-pH minimum and a matched, checksum-verified genome, spanning 429 bacterial families and 31 phyla, pH_min from 0.4 to 9.0 (mean 5.76).

| Source | Bacteria | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `literature_auto` | 2,536 | Growth ranges parsed from species-description abstracts, rule-based, validated against hand-curated rows |
| `thermobase` | 100 | ThermoBase v1.0 |
| `literature` | 90 | Hand-curated from species descriptions |
| `acidb` | 30 | AciDB, filtered to isolates with usable ranges |
| `bacdive` | 15 | BacDive |

Bound types are recorded (2,622 `measured`, 145 `unknown`, 4 `lowest_tested`) because a reported minimum is often only the lowest pH tested, not a demonstrated limit. Confidence tiers: 2,613 medium, 153 low, 5 high. Only 5 species have a *demonstrated* limit in the strict sense, which is the single biggest weakness of the phenotype data.

**The claim does not need AlphaFold** (`scripts/59`). Which side of the membrane a position faces comes from UniProt's curated topological domains; whether it is solvent-exposed comes from SASA on a predicted single-chain model. Recombining the per-class tallies on counts separates the two:

| Predictor | Estimate (per SD, composition controlled) |
|---|---|
| Periplasmic, **all** positions — topology only, no structure prediction | **+0.1871** (p 5.1e-10) |
| Periplasmic, exposed only — the published claim | +0.1848 (p 2.1e-10) |
| Periplasmic, buried only | +0.0370 (p 0.068) |
| Cytoplasmic, **all** positions — same treatment, as control | +0.0229 (p 0.32) |

Topology alone retains **101%** of the effect and the cytoplasmic side stays null, so the result rests on curated annotation rather than on a predicted structure — which makes the reference-structure noise above a threat to the exposure *refinement*, not to the result. It also corrects an emphasis this project has carried throughout: "acidic residues on the **exposed**, envelope-facing side" overstates what the exposure filter does. Per position it concentrates the signal about fivefold (+0.185 against +0.037 for the buried complement), but it adds nothing to the aggregate predictor, because halving the positions costs as much noise as the filter removes. **The specificity that matters is periplasmic against cytoplasmic, not exposed against buried.**

**The p-values are calibrated, and they survive it.** A phylogenetically autocorrelated predictor tested against a response whose covariance the model mis-specifies can reject far more often than nominally. Simulating the response from the null PGLS model with *no* effect, while the predictor keeps its real values and so its real phylogenetic structure (500 draws at each of three generating λ, testing at the fitted λ̂ = 0.572):

| Response generated at | Exposed periplasmic rejects at 0.05 | Soluble control rejects at 0.05 | Largest null \|t\| |
|---|---|---|---|
| λ̂ (model correct) | 6.2% | 5.0% | 3.06 / 3.82 |
| λ = 1 (Brownian; the realistic misspecification) | 7.2% | **13.0%** | 3.61 / 4.99 |
| λ = 0 | 5.4% | 2.4% | 2.71 / 3.06 |

Inflation for the composite is mild (1.2–1.4×) and nowhere near enough to explain the result: the observed |t| is **6.22** and the largest of 1,500 null draws is 3.61, so the finding lies beyond the entire simulated null. The control is the informative case — its nominal p of 0.015 (|t| 2.44) is unremarkable against a null that reaches 4.99 under misspecification, so the soluble surfaces are **null, not oppositely signed**. Holding λ fixed across simulations removes one source of null variability, which makes these rates optimistic about the test — the safe direction for this check.

**It can rank acidophiles on its own.** Discriminating pH_min ≤ 3 (76 of 1,655), family-blocked cross-validated AUC: composite alone **0.785**, genome-wide acidic excess 0.625, growth optimum 0.967, optimum + composite 0.969 (+0.0021, better in 19/20 fold assignments). So the composite is a usable standalone ranker where genome-wide composition is not, and still a rounding error beside the optimum.

The within-family test is the one that killed earlier leads (Section 4), and this is the only predictor class that passes it. **Both of those robustness rows now exist on the panel the headline rests on, and neither did until they were run** (`scripts/39a wide`, `scripts/39b wide`): both scripts already took a panel argument and had only ever been invoked with its default, so the headline's topology sensitivity and its family-fixed-effects estimate were untested while the hand-picked panel's were quoted in their place. An unused configuration option is a kind of undone work. Note the scale basis, which is this project's recurring trap: the family-fixed-effects figures are per SD of the **family-centred** predictor, which is the right scale for a within-family slope; on the raw composite's SD the same model gives +0.2052, and the two must never be mixed.

**Multiplicity, audited** (`scripts/68`). The project screened genome-wide features, then amino-acid composition, then structural position classes, and reports a result from the third. Counting *distinct hypotheses* rather than refits — 18 features, 21 residues, 7 position classes — gives **46**; the headline's p of 2.1e-10 clears Bonferroni at that count by **6.7 orders of magnitude**, and by 4.5 even against the indefensible count of all 7,526 p- or q-value rows in every result file. **But that reassurance is nearly worthless, and the same arithmetic shows why.** If multiplicity were the problem here, correction should have caught the three documented false leads. It catches two — histidine at 0.045 and Trk/Ktr at 0.034 — and misses the one that mattered: **Kdp reached p 1.17e-08 in the same screen, clearing the threshold by 5.0 orders of magnitude, and was withdrawn anyway.** What killed it was the within-family test and the evidence tier. So a sceptical reader should be directed at those tests rather than at the p-value, and the project's confidence should be located there too. Limitation: choices never recorded as a test — the position-count floor, which covariates enter a model, how a class is defined, which panel is primary — are not countable, and those are the forking paths that matter most. 46 is a lower bound on the search and the tests are not independent of one another.

**A confound found and disposed of: how well a genome aligns to the references** (`scripts/66`). Every per-genome value here comes from aligning that genome's protein to one of 32 *E. coli* reference structures, and a genome is scored on a reference only if enough of its positions align — so how many of the 32 it passes is a property of its divergence from *E. coli*. Coverage has been discussed throughout as a property of the *references*; as a property of the *genomes* it is a confound, and PGLS does not dispose of it, because distance to one particular organism is a direction on the tree rather than autocorrelation among tips. It is not hypothetical: under the headline's own model the count of references passing their floor gives **−0.171 ± 0.023 (p 2.8e-13)** and mean coverage fraction **−0.133 (p 7.3e-10)**. A pure pipeline property predicts the limit about as strongly as the biological claim does, which is a standing caveat on how much interpretive weight a coefficient of this magnitude deserves. It is nevertheless a *weak* confound, because it barely moves the predictor (composite against scorability r +0.086, against coverage r −0.112), and controlling both leaves the headline at **+0.180** against +0.185. Among the 270 genomes scoring on 28 or more references, where it hardly varies, the estimate is larger: +0.270, and +0.296 with the controls. The decisive test is specificity, since a pipeline artefact scores every position class through the same alignments and cannot be periplasm-specific. Controlling scorability and coverage moves **every class by at most 0.006**:

| Class | Published | + scorability + coverage | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exposed periplasmic | +0.1848 (p 2.1e-10) | **+0.1800** (p 3.9e-10) | −0.0048 |
| Exposed cytoplasmic | +0.0212 (p 0.33) | +0.0151 (p 0.48) | −0.0061 |
| Transmembrane | −0.0430 (p 0.073) | −0.0406 (p 0.087) | +0.0024 |
| Buried periplasmic | +0.0370 (p 0.068) | +0.0385 (p 0.056) | +0.0015 |
| Buried cytoplasmic | −0.0297 (p 0.13) | −0.0307 (p 0.12) | −0.0010 |

So the confound is disposed of rather than merely survived. There is also no upstream selection hiding in it: all 2,771 bacteria in the phenotype table are scorable on at least 3 references, so no genome was dropped for being too divergent.

**A second confound of the same family, with far more leverage, also disposed of** (`scripts/67`). The phenotype table has carried `assembly_type_material` since it was built and it was never tested, because it looked like bookkeeping. It is not: only **36** of 2,771 bacterial genomes are not from the species' type strain, but their mean pH_min is **2.62 against 5.80**, and they are **29.3% of the acid-tolerant tail** at pH_min ≤ 3 (24 of 82). They are the canonical acidophiles — *Acidithiobacillus*, *Leptospirillum*, *Acidiphilium*, *Methylacidiphilum*, *Sulfobacillus* — whose type strains are hard to culture, so the sequenced genome is more often a convenient strain. Since the association is one-sided and lives in that tail, 36 rows whose composite may have been computed on a different strain from the one whose pH was measured sat exactly where the result comes from, and that is predictor measurement error with no reason to be independent of the outcome. They do not carry it. The non-type indicator does not predict pH_min under the headline's model (**+0.022 ± 0.028, p 0.42**), so their low minima are explained by phylogenetic position rather than provenance; dropping all 36 moves the estimate **+0.1848 → +0.1815**; inside the tail (pH_min ≤ 4) it is +0.1498 with them and +0.1477 without; assembly level does nothing (+0.1852 with Contig/Scaffold/Chromosome indicators), as expected once scorability is accounted for; and specificity is unchanged under every treatment, with exposed cytoplasmic staying null and transmembrane slightly negative. Two limits on reading this. The within-tail figures are conditioned on the *outcome*, which restricts its range and attenuates the slope by construction, so +0.15 there is **not** comparable with the +0.29 that `scripts/63` gets by splitting on the predictor — the two are on different footings and should never be set side by side. And this tests only whether the result depends on those 36 rows, not whether their phenotypes are right; verifying each sequenced strain against the strain whose growth was measured needs two designations compared by hand against descriptions that are mostly paywalled. λ falls to 0 once family indicators are included, and a jackknife over families shows no single family carries it.

Bound type and curation confidence make no difference in either direction; database-only rows are same-signed but underpowered (+0.163, p 0.095). **But the familiar framing of this result has to go.** "The effect is strongest in the best tier" has been used here as a defence, and it is confounded: the hand-curated tier is heavily enriched for acidophiles — mean pH_min **3.66** against the parser's **5.93**, and **25.6%** at or below pH 3 against **0.5%** — and the relationship is one-sided, living almost entirely in the acid-tolerant tail. The larger estimate is what sample composition predicts with no appeal to data quality. Matching roughly on enrichment supports that: the database tier is also acidophile-rich (32.4% at or below pH 3) and gives +0.163, and the literature−database difference is z 1.63 (p 0.10). What the tier test establishes is that the lead **survives where measurement is best** — which is what it is for, and what the earlier leads failed.

The acidic term is unchanged in size with a p five orders of magnitude smaller, and the carrier is specifically **glutamate**: E is positive in 31 of 32 references, while aspartate is positive in 18 of 32 — a coin flip — so "D+E" names a unit of which only one half replicates. Arginine is inconsistent on both panels and the combined basic term is null on both. **What organises the five titratable residues is now measured rather than left as a negative result** (`scripts/90 wide`). Entered jointly, the per-point coefficients are D **+0.04049**, E **+0.10359**, H **−0.22047**, K **−0.01818**, R **+0.00740**: not monotone in pKa, acidic all positive, basic *mixed*. Three contrasts, each entered as its own coefficient so it carries a standard error, separate what is going on. Charge class is real and is **not** an artefact of histidine — the acidic pair differs from lysine and arginine by **+0.08681/pt** (p 1.6e−04) with histidine held fixed, against **+0.18477** when histidine is left inside the basic class, so a contrast against a group holding one large outlier mostly measures the outlier. pKa orders the acidic pair — glutamate over aspartate **+0.03958/pt** (p 3.9e−03) across a 0.6-unit gap, the direction a protonation account predicts. And histidine belongs to neither pattern: negative where its class-mates are null, **8.6×** their combined magnitude, and the reason the basic class is not sign-uniform. The per-pKa-unit rates are +0.032, +0.066 and −0.020, one reversed in sign, so no linear pKa slope fits all three. **Correction 18's pre-registered ordering therefore failed for two reasons, not one**: it was applied across charge classes, where charge sign dominates pKa, and it included a residue that does not behave like its class — which is correction 19's separate-co-varying-mechanisms finding reached from the residue side instead of the sidedness side. **The lysine term does not replicate** — per unit it is −0.0024 at p 0.10, not significant on this panel at all — and that matters beyond bookkeeping because it was half the rationale for the experiment's substitution (`scripts/74`): the design reverses D/E to K rather than neutralising, which earns its extra cost only if the basic residue contributes on its own. Expressed as a ratio in which the per-position scaling cancels, the margin reversal buys over simply removing the acid falls from **2.62× to 1.27×**. Nothing here says the substitution should change — whether K is easier to build or to interpret than an uncharged residue is a biochemical question this analysis cannot answer — but the margin must no longer be quoted from 7 references. One consequence is also *not* quantified on purpose: how much the primary arm's 5 aspartate positions cost it, since the only available arithmetic would mix coefficients from separately fitted univariate models (see correction 14).

**Distributed where there is anything to distribute — the unqualified claim is withdrawn** (`scripts/36c`, `scripts/36c wide`, `scripts/78`; correction 16). Splitting each protein's exposed periplasmic positions into random halves 40 times, the two halves should agree in sign in nearly every split if the effect is spread over many positions, in about half if one position carries it (each half gets that position half the time, however large the coefficient), and in a quarter to a third if there is no effect. On the hand-picked panel of 7 this looked settled: both halves positive in 92% of splits for YidC, 95% for NuoN and 85% for NuoL, against 30% for cytochrome *bo*₃. **On the 32 rule-selected references the median reference agrees in only 45% of splits and just 13 of 32 reach 80%**, so the claim cannot be stated flatly. It is conditional, and the condition is sharp:

| Subset of the 32 references | n | Median cross-half agreement | At ≥80% |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Individually significant (p<0.05)** | 10 | **100%** | **10/10** |
| Not individually significant | 22 | 35% | 3/22 |
| Coefficient ≤ 0 | 5 | 32% | 0/5 |

Agreement tracks whether a reference has an effect (r +0.788 against \|coefficient\|) and not how many positions it has (r +0.277) (`scripts/78`). That is not circular: a large coefficient cannot force agreement, because a singly-carried effect would agree in about half of splits at any effect size, so agreement near 100% means the effect survives being cut in half. The panel-wide 45% is therefore a restatement of `scripts/54`'s finding that most per-reference estimates measure scoring quality rather than biology, not evidence against distribution. **Exactly one reference shows the concentrated signature** — P62517, coefficient +0.032 with agreement at 38% across 51 positions — and is named rather than averaged into the median. The experiment's own targets sit in YidC, NuoL and NuoN, all of which pass strongly, so its premise that no single site carries the effect holds for the proteins it actually edits. Consistent with the per-position scan: of 250 positions, only 4 are polymorphic between acidic and basic at ≥15% each, and **no single position survives multiple-testing correction** (0/165 at q<0.1).

## 5. Corrections to my own analyses

Each of these changed a number I had already reported.

1. **SecY was carried by unreliable positions.** Requiring per-residue pLDDT ≥70 cut its exposed periplasmic class from 71 positions to 26 and the estimate from +0.16 (p<1e-8) to +0.04 (p 0.08). YidC (12% low-confidence) was unaffected. The original two-protein claim leaned on positions whose predicted exposure the model does not support.
2. **The "3× slope discrepancy" was my units error.** The within-family +0.31 was per *unit* of the composite and the between-species +0.10 per *standard deviation*; the composite's SD is 0.53, not 1. On one scale all estimators agree (+0.088 to +0.112). My statement that the within-family estimate was "probably inflated" was wrong and is withdrawn. I then made the same error again in the tree-sensitivity script and caught it only by arithmetic check.

4. **"My hand-picked panel was slightly favourable" — withdrawn, and reversed.** I reported that the rule-selected panel gave a smaller effect (+0.071/SD) than the hand-picked one (+0.101/SD) and called the smaller figure the honest headline. Both halves were wrong: the +0.071 was per unit, and the correct per-SD value is **+0.185**, roughly 1.8× the hand-picked panel's. Averaging 32 references instead of 7 reduces measurement error in the composite, so the standardized effect rises — which is what a real latent quantity should do, and is mild evidence *for* the finding rather than a caveat against it. **That explanation has since been tested rather than asserted** (`scripts/52`), with mixed results. Classical measurement error attenuates a slope by exactly the predictor's reliability, so β/reliability should be constant in panel size. Holding the sample fixed while panel size varies is severely constraining — reference coverage runs 21% to 100% and exactly *one* genome of 2,768 is scorable on all 32 — so it was run twice. On the 19 references at ≥80% coverage (n=455) it is **partly** met: β rises 2.46× from +0.045 at k=2 to +0.111 at k=16 while reliability rises 0.24→0.67, and correcting by √reliability leaves a residual rise of 1.46× (+0.093 to +0.136). So measurement error accounts for most of the panel-size effect but not all of it. On the 14 references at ≥85% coverage (n=979) β does not rise with k at all, so there is nothing to explain there. (A per-SD coefficient disattenuates by √reliability, not by reliability; I first divided by reliability, which over-corrected and made the residual rise vanish. `scripts/52` now carries a simulation of the correction that runs every time — see correction 7.) Neither configuration is well powered — complete-case restriction drops the whole-panel estimate to +0.124 (p 0.075) and +0.048 (p 0.27) — and they disagree. The relevant hard number either way: **mean correlation between references is only +0.09 to +0.11.** The references barely agree with each other, which is why reliability is low and why averaging many of them helps so much. **But there is one dominant common factor behind them** (`scripts/53`). Parallel analysis — each reference's column permuted independently, 500 draws — puts the first eigenvalue at 3.697 against a null 95th percentile of 1.444, far clear of it, while components 2 and 3 clear their nulls only marginally and 4 onward fall inside; the first explains 19.5% of the variance across 19 references, and **18 of 19 loadings are positive**, so the references do measure a common quantity the same way and the unweighted mean is not cancelling opposed structure. The low reliability reflects references of genuinely unequal quality, not an absent factor — and the first component, estimated without ever seeing the outcome, predicts the lower limit **better** than the unweighted mean the project has used throughout: **+0.244 against +0.198** per SD on all 2,768 (the two correlate 0.94), and with both in one model the mean falls to −0.017 (p 0.77) while the component holds +0.260. The component subsumes the mean entirely. **But it buys nothing where it would matter** (`scripts/56`): with the growth optimum also controlled the two are +0.117 and +0.112, a ratio of 1.05, and out of sample — loadings learned inside each training fold, so the predictor is one that could actually be deployed — the factor score adds +0.0071 of R² over the optimum against the unweighted mean's +0.0076. No better. So the "worth well under one percent of variance" conclusion is not an artefact of a lazily weighted predictor: correcting the attenuation raises the coefficient and does not raise how much of the limit the composite can explain beyond the optimum. Better weighting does help *ranking*: AUC for pH_min ≤ 3 is 0.844 for the unweighted mean of the 19 high-coverage references and 0.864 for the factor score, against 0.785 for the mean of all 32 — so **dropping the low-coverage references is worth +0.059 of AUC on its own, more than factor-weighting the rest is worth (+0.020)**.

6. **The per-unit against per-standard-deviation error recurred four times.** `pgls_scan` standardises its predictor internally, so its coefficient is already per SD; multiplying by the predictor's SD silently converts it back to per unit. I did that in the within-family test (correction 2), in the tree-sensitivity script, and in the wide-panel test (correction 4), each time reporting a number that was wrong by a factor of the composite's SD. 7. **I over-corrected for measurement error, and the over-correction flattered the explanation in correction 4.** Testing correction 4's claim required disattenuating a per-standard-deviation coefficient, and I divided by the predictor's reliability. The right divisor is its square root: with x = T + e, the per-*unit* slope attenuates by the reliability ρ, but a per-SD coefficient is b·sd(T)·√ρ, because the observed predictor's spread is itself inflated by the noise. Dividing by ρ over-corrects by a further 1/√ρ. With the right divisor the disattenuated estimate still rises 1.46× across panel sizes rather than staying flat, so measurement error explains most of the panel-size effect and not all of it. This is the fifth scale error of this family here, and the first caught by deriving the quantity rather than by noticing two numbers that disagreed; the script now simulates the correction on every run.

14. **The lysine term, and with it half the rationale for the experiment's substitution, was a 7-reference figure that does not replicate on 32.** This document reported "lysine specifically negative (−0.035, negative in 5/6)" alongside D+E at +0.041, so the basic half of a charge reversal looked almost as large as the acidic half, and `docs/CHARGE_REVERSAL_DESIGN.md` reverses D/E to K rather than neutralising on exactly that basis. `scripts/36c` had only ever been run on the hand-picked panel; on the rule-selected 32 the acidic term is intact (+0.040, p 4.1e-10, 30/32) while **lysine falls to −0.010 and is consistent in only 11 of 32 references** — and per percentage point, which is the scale this comparison needs, to −0.0024 at p 0.10, not significant on that panel at all. The scale-free margin that reversal buys over neutralisation therefore falls from **2.62× to 1.27×** (`scripts/74`; `scripts/73` first reported 1.86× to 1.26× on the mislabelled per-point scale of correction 15, which understated the gap). Two further things surfaced with it. The acidic claim's carrier is specifically **glutamate** (+0.038, 31/32) and not aspartate (+0.010, 18/32), so the eligibility criterion in `scripts/37b`, which treats D and E as interchangeably eligible, weights a component that does not replicate equally with one that does; the primary arm escapes this by being 9 glutamate to 5 aspartate, but by accident of ranking rather than by design. And **the cost of those 5 positions was deliberately not quantified at first**: the natural ratio — weighting each position by its own residue against treating all 14 as D+E — mixes coefficients from separate univariate fits, since `b_D` and `b_E` are not additive contributions summing to `b_DE`. Computed anyway it gives 0.63× on the hand-picked panel and 0.71× on the rule-selected one, below 1 on *both*, which is the tell that it measures the two models' incommensurability rather than aspartate's weakness. This was the **sixth** instance of this project's cross-model scale error and the first caught before the figure was published; `scripts/73` records the wrong number and why, on the view that a withheld figure is easier to re-derive by accident than a refuted one.

**It has since been quantified properly, and doing so corrected the framing above** (`scripts/75`). Entering D and E *simultaneously*, so each coefficient is that residue's effect holding the other fixed, is the legitimate version, and it is interpretable here because the two shares turn out to be nearly independent across genomes — median correlation −0.143, maximum variance inflation 1.16 — which is the precondition `scripts/73` could not check. Per percentage point, pooled across references: aspartate **+0.0050 (p 1.7e-4), positive in 23 of 32**, glutamate **+0.0119 (p 2.0e-11), positive in 32 of 32**, difference +0.0069 (p 0.002). So **"aspartate is a coin flip" was too strong and is withdrawn**: the univariate 18-of-32 was attenuated because the two shares are slightly *negatively* correlated and omitting glutamate biases the aspartate coefficient downward. Aspartate contributes; glutamate contributes about 2.4× as much, and that difference is real on this panel. The arm's weighting follows: glutamate supplies **81%** of its expected effect and the 5 aspartate positions **19%**, so the 14 positions are worth **11.1 glutamate-equivalents** — a 21% shortfall against counting them equally. And the quantity now behaves as a legitimate one should, which is the point: on the hand-picked panel the two residues are *not* distinguishable (+0.0076 against +0.0103, p 0.56) and the shortfall is only 9%, where the bogus cross-model version had been below 1 on both panels alike.

**One confound is specific to a comparison between two acidic residues, and nothing else in this project would have caught it.** Glutamate is GAA/GAG and aspartate GAU/GAC, so the two differ at the third codon position's GC content and a GC-rich genome is pushed toward aspartate; every other check here asks whether the acidic class matters at all, and none of them bears on which of the two carries it. The residue models control genome-wide acidic excess and source but **not GC**, so the asymmetry could have been codon usage. Adding GC and genome size to the same joint model (`scripts/75 wide`) leaves the difference at **+0.0061 (p 0.004)**, 89% of its uncontrolled size, with aspartate +0.0053 and glutamate +0.0114. So it is not a codon-GC artefact. On the hand-picked panel the difference is 83% of its uncontrolled size and remains non-significant (p 0.65), as it was before. Two incidental defects were fixed in the same pass: the pooled figures this document had been quoting were printed and never stored, so they were traceable to no file; and the source-indicator constancy screen was applied to the full sample rather than to the subset each fit actually runs on, which left the design singular on narrow references and had gone unnoticed because all 7 hand-picked references score nearly every genome.

(**The count was first recorded as seven**, from a grep for scripts that use `pgls_scan` and store a per-point column. Checking each by hand rather than trusting the grep removed `scripts/29`, which does divide: `beta / sd_h`. It is a second genuine exception alongside `scripts/32b`, which uses `ph.pgls` directly. So "+0.0336/pt" for the exposed periplasmic class, "+0.041/pt" for D+E, and every other per-point figure in this document were per SD of the relevant share, and the true per-point effects are **three to six times smaller** (`scripts/74`: pooled per unit, D+E is +0.0086 and the exposed periplasmic composite's components run 2.5 to 6.5 points of SD). **And one of the six carries correction 6's error as well, still live.** `scripts/34b` stores `beta_per_pt` = the scan's coefficient (per SD, mislabelled) *and* `beta_per_sd` = that coefficient **multiplied** by the SD — which is not per SD, nor per unit, but per unit times the SD squared. 16. **"The signal is distributed rather than carried by a few sites" was asserted without qualification from four references, and on 32 it is conditional.** The position-distribution test was the last claim in Section 3 still measured only on the hand-picked panel, where three of four references agreed across random halves in 85–95% of splits. 17. **The structural panel is not reproducible from its inputs, and the only reason anyone found out is that the rebuild was written to a new file.** Adding a histidine column to `scripts/36a` required rebuilding the 32-reference panel. Because that panel underlies every result here, the rebuild was deliberately written to `structure_panel_wide_h.csv` rather than over the original, which made a cell-for-cell comparison possible. Python randomises string hashing per process, so that order differs in every run and the output is a function of the process, not only of the inputs. **The effect on conclusions is nil**: fitting one common model to both builds gives +0.1762 against +0.1772 per SD, a difference of 0.036 of one standard error, so nothing is withdrawn. **That is not the reason to be reassured.** A figure that barely moves is still produced by a pipeline that is not a function of its inputs, and this project has already published once on "it barely moved" reasoning and had to withdraw it (correction 10). Identical cells, identical size, different bytes. C9 also caught itself twice, which is what its self-test is for: its first draft could not distinguish a set comprehension from a dict one and reported four dict comprehensions as nondeterministic, when dicts iterate in insertion order, and its coarse rule fired on two places where worker order reaches nothing written — those are exempted individually with the reason stated rather than switched off. 18. **The histidine refusal was right and its reason was wrong, and pooled, the pKa test runs and the pKa ordering fails.** `scripts/76` refused the mechanism test on a floor fixed before the data existed — the median reference must carry at least two histidines at exposed periplasmic positions, observed 0.00 — and the refusal was recorded here as "histidine is essentially absent from these surfaces". **As a compositional claim that is false.** Histidine is **1.69%** of exposed periplasmic positions, *higher* than the **1.22%** on the soluble controls' surfaces (`scripts/81 wide`). The median is zero because each reference contributes only about 31 such positions, so about 0.53 histidines are expected per reference per genome. The binding constraint was class **size**, never absence — and the remedy is the one this project already applies to every other residue: pool counts and positions across the 32 references before taking the share, which gives roughly 860 positions per genome. The distinction is not pedantic: "absent" closes a question, "too few positions per reference" names a fix, and **a refusal is only as good as its account of what is blocking it.** The floor itself is vindicated — nothing can be estimated per reference off counts of zero and one — so what failed was the diagnosis, not the discipline. **The pre-registered ordering then fails.** The prediction was fixed in `scripts/36a` and Section 8 before the histidine column existed and was genuinely two-sided: a charge-burden account predicts histidine (pKa ~6.0) behaves like a stronger glutamate, same sign and larger, since the group whose charge state turns over first as the envelope acidifies is the one that matters; a buffering account predicts the opposite sign, and this project's own withdrawn proteome-wide histidine lead ran that way. Entered together so each coefficient holds the others fixed: D **+0.0407/pt** (p 0.051), E **+0.1042/pt** (p 2.3e-9), H **−0.2207/pt** (p 4.2e-10) — **opposite sign at twice the magnitude.** **Two controls give opposite answers and both are reported.** It is **not** the withdrawn proteome-wide signal, which is the one confound worth naming here rather than a generic worry: the genome's own histidine share predicts nothing (p 0.96) and the surface share survives controlling it at **100%** of its uncontrolled size. But it **fails the specificity standard the acidic result passes** — the exposed cytoplasmic control is significant, same-signed, at **55%** of the periplasmic coefficient, where the acidic finding's cytoplasmic control is null (+0.019, p 0.38). *[Superseded the same week by correction 19: that control enters the cytoplasmic share alone and is mis-specified; histidine passes. The clause is left standing because it is what was reported, and because the reason it was wrong is the point of the correction that replaced it.]* A charge-burden mechanism should be strictly periplasmic, since that is where the proton gradient is, while buffering need not be, so the sign and the partial two-sidedness point the same way. **That is suggestive and is not proof**, and the tier this earns is a real association with an unsettled mechanism — neither a confirmed mechanism nor a null. What *is* settled is the question actually asked: **the residue ranking does not follow side-chain pKa monotonically.** One by-product is worth more than it looks: the pooled-share estimator returns D and E coefficients several times larger than `scripts/75`'s per-reference pooling, which reads as a contradiction and is correction 4's attenuation restated — a per-reference share is a noisier measure of the same quantity. The scale-free invariant replicates, **E/D = 2.56× here against 2.37× there, agreeing to 8%**, so the glutamate-over-aspartate ranking now holds under two independent estimators.
19. **The specificity control that decided correction 18 was mis-specified, and histidine passes it after all — the same omitted-variable error as correction 14, found inside a control instead of a headline.** `scripts/81 wide` reported exposed-cytoplasmic histidine at **−0.1223/pt (p 4.5e−4)**, significant and same-signed, concluded that correction 18's association is not specific to the outward-facing surface, and declined a D/E→H experimental arm on that basis. That control enters the cytoplasmic share **alone**, in a model carrying no periplasmic term at all, and the two shares correlate at **r 0.44** across genomes. A regressor entered without a correlate it shares variance with absorbs that correlate's signal: this is exactly correction 14's aspartate-without-glutamate, the ninth appearance of this family in the project and the first inside a *control*, where it is harder to look. Add the periplasmic share and the estimate moves **1.70 standard errors to −0.0632/pt (p 0.072)**; hold the cytoplasmic acidic and basic shares fixed as well and it reaches **−0.0473/pt (p 0.18)**. The discriminating test is not the verdict but the *distance moved*, which the correlation predicts in advance: the acidic measure, correlated at only r 0.17, moves **0.34 SE** — a factor of 4.9, in the predicted direction. **The null is marginal, not clean,** and is not described in the same words as the acidic control's p 0.98. The positive claim does not depend on that borderline call. Entering both shares in one fit as **sum and difference** makes the periplasmic excess an estimate with a standard error of its own — **−0.0800/pt, p 0.0031** — on a contrast *calibrated* against the acidic result, whose own difference fires at **+0.0536/pt, p 3.3e−6**: a test is only a test once it has been shown it can fire. So there are two components, a shared one and a side-specific one. What this does **not** touch is correction 18's periplasmic coefficient, its sign, or the failure of the pKa ordering — all three came from a fit that already held the other residues fixed. What falls is the stated reason for declining the histidine arm, and the shared component is now shared with something that is not the cytoplasmic face and has not been identified. `scripts/82 wide` also records three of its own first drafts that would each have been reported as a result: a shift measured as a percentage of a baseline that is itself zero, which said the predicted ordering had failed; a rank-deficient fit for the acidic measure, whose cytoplasmic regressor *is* cytoplasmic D+E, printed with a standard error of 168457 and p 1.00; and a docstring claim that sum/difference is the better-conditioned parametrisation, true for histidine (r 0.23 against 0.44) and markedly false for the acidic measure (0.62 against 0.17) because the two sides' spreads differ by 2.05×.

20. **The headline acidic coefficient is inflated by about 27% by an omitted correlate on the same surface — correction 14's error reaching the headline measure itself.** Exposed-periplasmic histidine entered the panel only after correction 18, so no figure reported here had ever held it fixed. Held fixed, the exposed-periplasmic acidic share goes from **+0.10681 to +0.07745 per point** (p 5.9e−09), and `scripts/84 wide` part 9 decomposes it: the basic residues account for 1%, so almost all of the 27% is histidine. The direction was predicted before any model was fitted rather than read off one — the two shares correlate **−0.549** with opposite-signed coefficients, so a genome high in exposed acidic residues is low in exposed histidine and both departures push the limit the same way, which means a model omitting histidine lets the acidic share carry part of histidine's effect. An assertion in `scripts/87 wide` requires the fit to shrink, as that account demands. This is the fourth appearance of the omitted-correlate error in three days: in a headline (14), in a control (19), in a new script (`scripts/83 wide`), and now in the headline *measure*. **What it is not is a proxy relationship**, and the test that separates those uses a property the two measures are already known not to share. Under mutual adjustment neither displaces the other — the acidic share keeps 73%, histidine 77%, both at p<1e−8 — where a proxy would leave one near zero; and the acidic effect's defining property, strict one-sidedness, **survives** holding histidine fixed at 77% of its size (+0.05360 → +0.04106, p 4.0e−04), where correction 19 found histidine itself carries a large component on *both* faces. So they are separate mechanisms that co-vary, and the 27% is shared variance the specification was crediting entirely to the acidic share. **Scope, stated narrowly.** This does not touch the published per-SD headline of +0.185: that comes from `scripts/57`'s model on a different composite, and comparing the two would itself be correction 12. What it does affect is every per-point figure for the exposed-periplasmic acidic share, including the ones added to this document and the README earlier the same day. It also makes the composite a live question rather than a settled one: `net D+E−K−R` gives **+0.2342 per SD** of that composite and `net D+E−K−R−H` gives **+0.2924**, a 25% increase. That change is **not adopted** — the composite is fixed in `docs/PREREGISTRATION.md` and amending it belongs to the deviations procedure, not to a script — and `scripts/87 wide` exists to give that decision its number.
21. **I reported that the acidic prediction had no supporting experiment, on the strength of a search capped at three results — and the paper the cap hid is the most relevant one in the project.** [`EXPERIMENTAL_EVIDENCE.md`](EXPERIMENTAL_EVIDENCE.md) was written from a semantic search that returns three papers per query, and concluded that nobody had manipulated periplasmic acidic residues in a bacterial membrane protein and measured acid resistance. An exhaustive PubMed query on the same question returns **59** records, and one of them is Weeks & Sachs 2001 (PMID 11442825), which mutagenised **every** histidine, aspartate and glutamate in the periplasmic domain of the *H. pylori* urea channel UreI — the exhaustive periplasmic titratable-residue scan this project's prediction is about — and found His-123, His-131, His-193, **Asp-129, Asp-140 and Glu-138** needed to activate transport at acidic pH, with those residues largely absent from the pH-independent *S. salivarius* homologue. **The error was methodological, not clerical:** the claim was hedged as "not found by these queries", and the hedge was true, but an absence claim drawn from a three-result search is not an absence claim. The lesson is the one the checker already enforces for figures and nothing enforced for literature — state what the search could have found, not what it did. **What the paper does to the substance is more interesting than the correction.** It confirms the compartment twice over, and **contradicts the direction**: in UreI periplasmic carboxylates are required for low-pH function, where this project finds them depleted in acid-tolerant genomes. Those are different quantities — a dedicated gate in one specialised channel against bulk composition over 32 housekeeping proteins — and §2.2 of that document makes the reconciliation explicitly: a protein that must *respond* to acid needs titratable residues on the sensing face, while a proteome that must merely *function* at low pH may do better with fewer. But the direct evidence now favours **buffering or function** over the charge-burden mechanism this project has been offering, which is a claim about mechanism and not about the association. The association is untouched. The explanation attached to it is weaker than it was this morning.

## 6. What the evidence does not show

- **Causality is untested.** On the hand-picked panel the sister-pair test across 844 pairs was null (52.0% in the predicted direction) and I concluded that no observational test on recent natural substitutions could settle this. **That conclusion was wrong and is withdrawn.** On the wide panel the predicted direction holds in 56.5% of 600 sister pairs (q 0.0007). Specificity needed one further step: the sister-pair test, unlike the regressions, did not control genome-wide composition, and until it did the exposed *cytoplasmic* class was positive too; residualising composition before differencing leaves exposed periplasmic the only class surviving correction. So recent substitutions do carry the signal in the predicted direction. This is still not causal evidence — the substitutions were not assigned — but it is a much stronger observational test than a cross-species slope, and only the experiment addresses causality.
- **Archaea are consistent with bacteria but still marginal on their own** (`scripts/43`). Redone on the wide panel, where 15 references pass 60% archaeal coverage against the single membrane reference available before: with halophiles excluded (n=177) the exposed extracellular composite gives **+0.153 per SD (p 0.065)**, not significant but only 0.4 archaeal standard errors from the bacterial +0.185 (`scripts/57`), against a detectable effect of 0.230 — so it cannot exclude zero either. Over all 318 with a halophile indicator it is **+0.237 (p 0.001)** with every matched control null (cytoplasmic −0.111, buried −0.007, transmembrane +0.025, soluble surface +0.039), a much cleaner specificity pattern than the earlier three-reference test, where the soluble control was as large as the signal. Within the 141 halophiles the extracellular-positive, cytoplasmic-negative pattern holds (+0.175 and −0.136) but transmembrane is also positive (+0.197), so that subset still lacks specificity and remains confounded with alkaliphily. Open sources are exhausted at the abstract level. **One route reopened on 2026-11-01** (`scripts/94`): metaTraits' measured-only export carries 4,323 species absent from this project, 51 at `ph_min` <= 3.0, and its acid end is heavily archaeal — *Picrophilus*, *Thermoplasma*, *Sulfolobus*, *Metallosphaera*, *Saccharolobus*, *Acidilobus*. It is a curation route and not data: the export is species-level where this project's unit is a strain with an assembly, 75% of the new rows rest on a single observation, and bound type is as absent as ever, so every row would enter at confidence `low` and needs a strain and a primary source first.
- **Heterogeneity is high.** I² 65–68% between references, positive in 27 of 32. This is a tendency across conserved membrane proteins, not a property each one has.
- **Most of the association runs through the growth optimum, but less of it than this document used to say** (`scripts/57`). Fitted on the same 1,655 genomes — those whose optimum is known — the composite gives **+0.2123** per SD with composition controlled and **+0.0904** with the optimum added, so **57%** of the association is attributable to the optimum and **43%** is independent of it. **Holding exposed histidine fixed as well leaves 39%** (`scripts/89 wide`, correction 20): the measure is **+0.03502** per point, **+0.1120** per SD, p 0.0039, and the two controls are close to independent — 38% as the product of their separate shares against 39% jointly — so each removes variance the other does not. The earlier "roughly two-thirds, leaving +0.042/SD" was wrong twice over: +0.042 was a per-unit figure already withdrawn under correction 6 yet still quoted here, and the two-thirds came from comparing the optimum row against the **full-sample** +0.1848, which differs in sample as well as model. The optimum-known subsample has a *larger* composition-only estimate than the full sample, which is exactly why that comparison overstates the optimum's share. See correction 12.
- **Structural annotation does not transfer between reference structures, and this is now measured rather than assumed.** Topology and exposure come from single-chain AlphaFold models of *E. coli* (and one *B. subtilis*) references, applied to all species by alignment. The old reassurance — two SecY reference species, coefficients +0.042 and +0.071, difference p 0.42 — compared *coefficients*, which agree, and not *measurements*, which do not. Scoring the same protein in the same genome against different reference structures of its own Pfam family gives correlations of only **+0.252** (NuoL vs NuoN), +0.079 and +0.087 (each against HyfB) and **+0.126** (SecY *E. coli* vs *B. subtilis*) — median **+0.126** (`scripts/55`). The pipeline is not at fault: the same accession scored through both panels returns r = 1.000 and 0.999. So a per-genome exposed-periplasmic value is largely specific to the reference it was scored against, the aggregate result is whatever survives averaging over that noise, and no single protein's estimate should be read as biology. On *coefficients* the references still do not differ by more than chance (0 of 7 pairs; NuoL/NuoN against HyfB at p 0.055 and 0.051).
- **A reference's own coefficient mostly measures how cleanly it can be scored.** Across 31 references the raw per-reference coefficient correlates **+0.817** with that reference's reliability (`scripts/54`). **Partly, but only partly, because bigger references are measured with less noise** (`scripts/72`). A genome's value for a reference is an average over its exposed periplasmic positions, which run 17 to 262 across the panel, so Spearman-Brown predicts larger references score higher on reliability for purely topological reasons. Position count does correlate +0.572 with reliability, and a one-parameter measurement-averaging model predicting reliability from count alone explains **R² 0.346** of the ranking — a third, not most of it. The +0.817 association falls only to **+0.737** with log position count residualised out of both, so this bullet's conclusion stands largely on its own terms and is not reducible to protein length. Only 8 reach reliability 0.20; above that floor all 8 are positive and their noise-corrected estimates correlate +0.80 with the factor loadings from `scripts/53`, which is the coherent part of the picture. Below it, disattenuation is division by near-zero and produces nonsense, so no full ranking of the 32 is reported.
- **The phenotype data are mostly parser-extracted, and that is now quantified** (`scripts/60`). 2,623 of 2,768 bacterial minima come from rule-based extraction of species-description abstracts. Against the hand-curated rows, on the gate actually in use, the parser is **exact for 98.8%** of minima (mean error −0.004, SD 0.033, largest error 0.30), so transcription is not the weakness. But that validation was run on the abstracts that had been hand-curated, which are acidophile-heavy — mean minimum 3.38, 34% at or below pH 3 — against 5.93 and 0.5% in the rows actually used. Hand-reading 25 random rows from the range that carries the data (pH_min 4–7) closed that gap and found **3 of 25 wrong, all the same failure**: a sentence reporting only an optimum ("grew optimally at 35 °C, at pH 7.0–8.0") has its optimum recorded as the growth range, biasing the minimum upward. The failure is mechanically detectable, and `scripts/61` detects it exactly: an "optim" stem just before the parser's range match, a matched interval no wider than 2 pH units (an optimum spanning four units is not an optimum), no optimum following, and no independent range clause. Tested against 15 statements read by hand and recorded verbatim with their verdicts, it scores **8 of 8 with no false positives and no misses**. It flags **298 of 2,536 parser rows (11.8%)** — matching the independent 3-in-25 hand-check rate — whose mean reported minimum is **6.81 against 5.82**, a full pH unit of upward bias, and within which the effect is attenuated to +0.083 (p 0.056). **Dropping exactly those rows moves the headline from +0.1848 to +0.1886, which is 0.13 SE.** So a quantified, mechanistically understood error in 12% of the phenotypes has no material effect on the result. (An earlier version of this entry used a broader proxy — all 1,097 rows with no parsed optimum — and reported that dropping them moved the estimate to +0.217, calling the published figure conservative by 1.1 SE. That was a sample-composition artefact of dropping 1,097 rows when only 201 were affected, and is withdrawn; see correction 10.) The recommended data fix, not yet applied because it changes the phenotype table, is to mark those 298 rows' bound type rather than trusting their minima.
- **The outcome's own reliability is now measured, and it does not excuse the small variance share** (`scripts/62`). Four source tables — the hand-curated tier, ThermoBase, AcidB and BacDive — overlap on 114 species, so those species carry two or more curators' readings of the same organism; the phenotype table's source-precedence rule keeps one and hides the rest. A one-way decomposition gives the reliability of a single reported pH_min as **0.965** on the replicate set and **0.905** against the bacterial sample's own spread, i.e. a curator error of about **0.34 pH units**. That sets a ceiling of 0.905 on explainable variance, against which the composite's out-of-sample contribution of **0.0076** is still small — so poor measurement of the outcome is not why the predictive claim is modest, and the modest claim stands as written. Error in the outcome does not bias the slope at all; the script simulates that rather than asserting it, because the reflex to disattenuate for it is the same reflex that produced correction 7. **Two limits on how far this can be pushed.** The four curators are not independent measurements but largely independent *transcriptions* of a shared primary literature, so every figure is an upper bound: the growth experiment's own error, the medium, the temperature at which pH was read and the strain's identity are common to all of them and cancel out. And the replicated species are acidophile-heavy by construction (median 3.4, only 34 bacteria), so the headline was not refitted on them. One by-product: measured against the hand-curated tier, the precedence ordering is arguably backwards — BacDive strays least (SD 0.080) and sits last, AcidB strays most (0.410) and sits above it — but the bootstrap intervals overlap and only 15 genomes turn on that call, so nothing is made of it.
- **Only 5 species have a demonstrated limit in the strict sense** — a reported minimum is usually the lowest pH at which growth was seen, not a shown boundary. This is untouched by everything above and remains the single biggest weakness in the phenotype data. **One route to upgrading it is now closed, and the closure is worth recording** (`scripts/65`). Since most species descriptions are paywalled, the idea was to probe Europe PMC's index rather than fetch text — ask whether a paper contains "no growth at pH 4.0" without reading it. It appeared to work, screening 205 of 2,708 papers positive and giving +0.52 per SD on that subset. It was entirely an artefact: `"no growth at pH 4.0"`, `"growth at pH 4.0"` and `"not growth at pH 4.0"` all return the identical 114 hits while `"never growth at pH 4.0"` returns 0, because the index drops *no* and *not* as stopwords, so every negated query matched the affirmative statement. Checked against ground truth the repository already holds: of 201 screened-positive papers whose statement is stored here, **2 contain a failure-to-grow clause of any kind**. Nothing from it is reported, and no earlier script used a negated index query. Separately, the 34 open-access papers whose full text *was* fetched and parsed locally (`scripts/00h`–`00i`, where the parse does read the negation) map to only **2** genomes in the analysis table, both already hand-curated as `measured` — so that harvest was fully exploited and the figure of 5 is near-exhaustive for the sources available. The gap needs full text that does not exist openly, or hand-reading.

## 7. The experiment

**The analysis is pre-registered** in `docs/PREREGISTRATION.md`, written before any strain was built, because twelve of the corrections in Section 5 are post-hoc flexibility of the kind a pre-registration prevents and bench work cannot be re-run cheaply. It commits to one primary endpoint and one test, with the dose series, six controls, reverse arm and pooled assay all secondary under correction across 17 declared comparisons; to **n ≥ 64 per group**, powered for the conservative −0.05 prediction rather than the design's −0.16, since the design's n ≥ 20 floor detects only 0.091 pH units and is underpowered 1.8× on that scaling (`scripts/70`); to three gates with pre-specified stop conditions, including measuring the assay SD, which is unsourced and decides feasibility, and the fitness-per-pH-unit slope the pooled assay needs and nobody has measured; and to a decision table in which a null at insufficient depth is declared **uninformative** rather than evidence against. It also declares in advance what a null would establish: because annotation transfers between reference structures at a median correlation of only +0.126 (`scripts/55`), a null in NuoL/NuoN bounds those 14 positions in those two proteins and little else, so a positive result is strong evidence and a null is weak — a property of the design, not a defence built after seeing one.

`docs/CHARGE_REVERSAL_DESIGN.md`. Prediction: removing exposed-periplasmic negative charge (D/E→K) should *lower* pH_min.

- **Primary strain:** NuoL + NuoN double, all 14 selected positions, predicted **−0.16 pH units**. No single protein suffices (saturating one gives −0.04 to −0.10).
- **Readout:** pooled barcoded competition. The assay's resolution must be *demonstrated* (spike-in ladder) before variants are interpreted; n≥20 for isolated-strain confirmation.
- **Controls** are chosen so the hypothesis predicts a different outcome for each: charge-neutral intermediate, same-charge null, matched cytoplasmic and buried null.
- **Declared asymmetry:** a null would bound the per-protein contribution but would *not* refute the comparative finding, since the signal is an aggregate over many proteins. This is a real weakness of the design.

Environment variables `DATA_DIR` and `PHYLO_DIR` select the dataset; `PANEL_CSV` selects the structural panel. `scripts/69` is a staleness check on this document: it pulls every signed coefficient out of the prose and looks for it in the result file that the same claim cites, which is possible because each script declares its output in its first docstring line. Its own weakness is measured every run — a made-up coefficient matches a cited file 0.7% of the time at five decimal places, 2.2% at four, 7.1% at three and 23.6% at two, against 42–100% if matched against every result file at once, so restricting to the cited script is what gives the check any power. It is mutation-checked. **It also measures its own FALSE-MISS exposure**, which the chance-match rate does not: five mechanical ways a correct figure goes unverified have been found and are now counted — a citation without its panel argument, a declared output absent from the dataset, a figure quoted to record a rejected draft, a citation written without the `scripts/` prefix, and an output declared as a literal `results/` path rather than through `DATA_DIR`. The last two were found by reading rather than by any check. **It had also been verifying figures against its own record of them.** Its output logs every figure it examined in a `quoted` column — 617 numbers, every coefficient these documents assert — so pooling that file meant any claim citing `scripts/69` had its numbers matching themselves. Excluding its own output, and the audit's, exposed five figures passing on that alone, every one a real problem. It was found by recording a **per-figure** chance-match rate rather than a corpus average: median **3.1%**, but 30 figures above 50%, where a pass says almost nothing. The average answers "how much is a clean run worth"; only the per-figure rate answers "is *this* claim verified". One class of high rate is deliberately not a defect — a round design target is not a measurement, and adding digits to it would invent precision. **And a claim that cites nothing cannot fail**, which the per-document "unsourced" count had been absorbing: across all four documents **34** distinct figures are now checked in no document at all, against 71 before this was measured, and 12 of those sit in the corrections section that is exempt by design — the exemption that makes the check possible is also its largest hole. It cannot tell whether a figure is the *right* one for its claim, only that it still exists somewhere it should. **A fourth layer was added on 2026-11-01: the estimator itself, reproduced by an implementation sharing no code with it** (`scripts/95`). Twenty corrections have been found in the analyses and none in the estimator, and until this ran, the reason might have been that nothing had looked: a bug in `pgls` would produce a plausible coefficient with a plausible standard error, and every robustness check in the project would inherit it, because every one of them calls it. The existing tests cannot see this — they check internal identities, and `vcv_from_tree` and `vcv_fast` both take their branch lengths from Bio.Phylo and both define covariance through the same MRCA notion, so their agreement tests the traversal and not the definition. The independent implementation uses its own recursive-descent Newick parser, builds the covariance from shared root-to-tip path length by longest-common-prefix comparison rather than a tree walk, and fits by symmetric eigendecomposition, explicit normal equations and a 4,001-point λ grid — no Cholesky, no `lstsq`, no Brent search, no Cholesky log-diagonal determinant. **All 30 comparisons agree**, 10 to floating point and 20 inside declared tolerances: the headline at **+0.184838 per SD against the published +0.184837**, the cytoplasmic and transmembrane controls, and both sided results to 2e−7 per point. The scope is narrow and stated in the script: this reproduces the fitting and the predictor's arithmetic from stored intermediates, not the structural panel, the HMM detection, the exposure calls or the phenotype curation — an error in any of those is invisible to it. `pytest tests/` covers the library: 26 tests, including the per-SD/per-unit identity that five scale errors here violated and the optimum-as-range detector against all fifteen hand-read statements. Both were mutation-checked — returning per-unit from `pgls_scan`, and dropping the detector's width condition, each fail — because a test that cannot fail is not a guard.
