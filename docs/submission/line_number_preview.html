10Abstract
11Background. The genomic correlates of reported minimum growth pH may differ from those of growth
12optimum. We tested whether charge composition on the outward-facing surfaces of conserved
13membrane proteins is associated with reported minimum growth pH after adjustment for growth
14optimum.
15Methods. We analysed 2,768 bacterial genomes using a 32-reference membrane-protein panel,
16structural confidence and solvent-accessibility filters, and phylogenetic generalised least
17squares with genome-wide composition and source controls. Matched cytoplasmic and transmembrane
18positions provided compartment controls. Models incorporating growth optimum used the same 1,655
19genomes for each comparison.
20Results. The extracytoplasmic net acidic composite was positively associated with reported
21minimum growth pH (+0.1848 pH units per standard deviation; P = 2 × 10⁻¹⁰). The cytoplasmic and
22transmembrane controls were not statistically significant. A separate pooled residue-share
23analysis adjusted for growth optimum and exposed histidine retained 39% of its own baseline
24association (+0.1120 per standard deviation; P = 4 × 10⁻³). Seven of 22 detectable reference
25families passed Benjamini–Hochberg correction at q < 0.10. The composite did not improve
26prediction in held-out phyla.
27Conclusions. Acid-tolerant bacteria have lower net acidic composition on extracytoplasmic-facing
28positions of this reference panel. The result is an observational association; most reported
29minima are not experimentally demonstrated boundaries. A charge-burden interpretation is a
30hypothesis requiring experimental testing.
31Introduction
32Bacteria differ greatly in the acidity at which growth is observed. Some Acidithiobacillus
33isolates grow below pH 1; the archaeal genus Picrophilus also includes organisms capable of
34growth at very low pH. A reported minimum growth pH is distinct from the growth optimum. A
35species with an optimum near pH 7 may have reported growth extending to pH 4 or only to pH 6.
36Here we study that reported minimum, without assuming it is a demonstrated biological boundary.
37The mechanisms of acid resistance that are understood in detail are inducible and physiological.
38Enteric bacteria surviving gastric transit deploy amino-acid decarboxylase systems that consume
39cytoplasmic protons, regulated by sensors that detect acidification directly: the EvgS/EvgA two-
40component system, in which a single histidine in the extracytoplasmic domain is required for
41acid induction [1], and the regulator AdiY, whose pH response runs through the protonation of
42two histidines [2]. Helicobacter pylori additionally imports urea through the pH-gated channel
43UreI to feed intrabacterial urease [3]. Experimental evolution for acid resistance in
44Escherichia coli converges on the same signalling machinery, with evgS mutated in every selected
45population [4]. These are responses: machinery switched on when the environment turns acidic,
46and they explain survival of acid shock better than they explain the pH at which steady growth
47becomes impossible.
48A limit might instead be set by what the cell's proteins can tolerate, and if so the relevant
49compartment is identifiable in advance. A Gram-negative bacterium holds its cytoplasm near
50neutrality even in strong acid, so cytoplasmic proteins never experience the external pH. The
51periplasm has no such protection: it equilibrates far closer to the medium, and proteins with
52surfaces facing it operate at whatever pH the organism is growing in. Any compositional
53signature of acid adaptation should therefore appear on outward-facing surfaces and not on
54cytoplasmic ones — which makes the hypothesis testable against a control that is the same
55measurement on a different face of the same protein. Consistent with the compartment mattering,
56extracytoplasmic redox proteins of acidophiles have systematically more positive redox
57potentials than neutrophilic homologues [5].
58Compositional signatures of other extremes are established; for pH they are not. Thermophily is
59visible in the IVYWREL fraction and halophily in a pronounced excess of surface acidic residues,
60while a review of archaeal extremophile protein adaptation reports that adaptations toward pH
61are considerably less discernible [6]. That is a documented absence rather than an assumption,
62and it is the gap addressed here.
63Two recent results make the question tractable. Cabello-Yeves et al. showed that predicted
64isoelectric point shifts with habitat between marine and freshwater prokaryotes, with the
65difference sharper in secreted than cytoplasmic than membrane proteins and concentrated on
66protein surfaces [7], establishing that compartment-resolved composition carries environmental
67signal. Independently, Barnum et al. showed that growth pH can be predicted from amino acid
68composition computed per predicted subcellular localization, reporting the extracellular-minus-
69intracellular glutamate difference as the strongest residue-level correlate [8]. Together these
70say that the compartment-resolved signal exists and that pH is among the variables it carries.
71We use extracytoplasmic to describe the outward-facing side of the cytoplasmic membrane across
72the bacterial sample. In diderm bacteria these positions face the periplasm; in monoderms they
73face the cell wall and extracellular environment. Position classes are transferred from
74reference annotations by sequence alignment, rather than measured structurally in each sampled
75organism.
76We test which genomic features are associated with reported minimum growth pH after adjustment
77for growth optimum, and whether the association differs between outward-facing and cytoplasmic
78positions of the same proteins.
79Materials & Methods
80Phenotype data
81The analysis sample is 2,768 bacterial genomes carrying a curated minimum growth pH and a
82position in the reference phylogeny. Minima were compiled from four source tiers, recorded per
83record: hand-curated species descriptions read from primary sources, rule-based extraction from
84species-description abstracts, ThermoBase, and AciDB/BacDive [9]. Source is entered as a set of
85indicator covariates in every model, so a between-source level difference cannot by itself
86produce an association.
87The extraction tier dominates the sample and its fidelity was measured rather than assumed.
88Against hand-curated records it is exact for 98.8% of minima. Because that validation set was
89acidophile-enriched, 25 further records were read by hand from the pH band carrying most of the
90data; 3 of 25 were wrong, all by one mechanism, in which a sentence reporting only an optimum
91has that optimum recorded as the growth range. A detector for that specific failure scores 8 of
928 against fifteen hand-read statements with no false positives and flags 298 of 2,536 extracted
93records; excluding exactly those records moves the headline estimate by 0.13 standard errors.
94One assembly was retained per species. The bacterial phenotype table contains 2,771 assemblies;
95intersection with the reference phylogeny produces the 2,768-genome headline sample. Before that
96intersection, source tiers contain 2,536 automatically extracted records, 100 ThermoBase
97records, 90 hand-curated records, 30 AciDB records and 15 BacDive records. Sources can overlap;
98the deposited table records the selected source and assembly provenance. The hand-curated tier
99is enriched for acidophiles and is not a representative random validation set.
100The 25-record central-range check found three optimum-as-range extraction errors. The targeted
101detector was assessed on 15 hand-read statements and flagged 298 extracted records. The primary
102analysis retains the deposited phenotype table; exclusion of flagged records is a sensitivity
103analysis, not a correction of those records. Only five species were recorded as having a
104strictly demonstrated lower limit in the project curation. Cross-source agreement largely
105measures agreement in transcription of shared literature and does not establish the precision of
106the original growth experiments.
107Structural panel and composite
108The predictor is built on a panel of conserved inner-membrane proteins of Escherichia coli with
109experimentally determined or high-confidence predicted structures. Each reference position was
110assigned to a class — exposed extracytoplasmic, exposed cytoplasmic, transmembrane or buried —
111from UniProt topology together with relative solvent accessibility of the isolated model,
112computed by the Shrake–Rupley algorithm [18] and normalised to the maxima of Tien et al. [10],
113with exposure defined as RSA ≥ 0.25. A position enters only if its per-residue pLDDT is ≥ 70,
114since the solvent accessibility of a poorly predicted residue is not interpretable. For each
115genome, the best-aligning hit of the reference's Pfam family was tallied by the class of the
116reference position it aligned to, counting D, E, K, R and H separately.
117The composite is the mean across references of the per-reference net acidic share (D + E − K −
118R) divided by scorable positions, each reference standardised across genomes before averaging,
119with a per-reference position floor and a minimum of three contributing references. The panel
120used throughout is a 32-reference rule-selected set; a 7-reference hand-picked panel preceded it
121and is not the same measurement, so per-unit coefficients from the two are never compared.
122Canonical and deterministic panel provenance is distinguished below; the canonical stored panel
123is not a byte-identical rebuild of the corrected code.
124Reference selection used reviewed E. coli K-12 proteins with at least four transmembrane
125helices, at least three annotated outward-facing and three cytoplasmic topological domains, and
126exactly one Pfam family. One candidate per family was retained using the number of annotated
127topological domains. Screening used 150 sampled bacterial proteomes, an 80% detection threshold,
128median model pLDDT of at least 70, and at least 15 exposed outward-facing positions after
129residue-confidence filtering. Accepted reference identifiers and annotations are deposited in
130data/curated/structure_panel_refs.csv; five conserved soluble references serve as controls.
131For the headline composite, a reference contributes only when the number of scorable positions
132reaches max(8, half the reference class length). Its net acidic share is (D + E − K − R)/n. Each
133reference share is centred and scaled across the available genomes, then available standardised
134shares are averaged; at least three references must contribute. Model rows are restricted to
135complete outcomes, predictors and covariates; missing growth optima are not imputed. The genome-
136wide composition control is acidic excess, (D + E − K − R), expressed as percentage points.
137Source indicators use hand-curated literature as the reference category.
138A separate analysis pools counts across reference proteins, requires at least 200 outward-facing
139exposed positions, and models net residue share together with exposed histidine. It is a
140different predictor from the mean of standardised reference shares. Retained percentages are
141calculated within that pooled-share analysis and must not be computed by dividing its
142coefficient by a coefficient from the headline composite. The exact model samples, scales,
143coefficients and standard errors are recorded in headline_numbers.csv and
144optimum_and_histidine_wide.csv.
145Statistical model
146All estimates are phylogenetic generalised least squares on a Brownian covariance matrix derived
147from the reference marker-gene tree, with Pagel's λ [11] estimated by maximum likelihood. Every
148model carries an intercept, a genome-wide amino acid composition control and the source
149indicators. Coefficients are reported per standard deviation of the predictor unless a per-unit
150figure is explicitly labelled, because composites averaging different numbers of references have
151different spreads. Where a model adds the growth optimum, the comparison is made on the single
152sample for which the optimum is known, never against the full-sample figure, so that a change of
153model is not confounded with a change of sample. Multiple testing across reference families is
154controlled by the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure [12].
155Compartment contrasts are estimated by entering both faces in one model as their sum and
156difference. This spans the same column space as entering the two levels and so changes no fitted
157value, but it makes the extracytoplasmic excess an estimate carrying its own standard error,
158which a difference taken between two separately fitted coefficients does not.
159Phylogeny construction and sensitivity
160The phylogeny uses 29 ribosomal-protein Pfam marker families. Highest-scoring gathering-cutoff
161hits were aligned to their Pfam profiles with pyHMMER hmmalign using match columns and
162concatenated. Genomes with fewer than half the retained families and columns with less than 50%
163occupancy were removed. Gamma-corrected Poisson distances on shared non-gap sites were used for
164neighbour joining, followed by midpoint rooting. The marker list, assembly order and retained
165tree are deposited with the code. The Brownian covariance is shared root-to-tip path length;
166Pagel’s λ scales its off-diagonal entries and is estimated by maximum likelihood.
167The headline association remained significant on 25 column-bootstrap trees, with coefficients
168ranging from +0.1751 to +0.1863 per standard deviation (median +0.1822). These trees share an
169alignment and inference method. No maximum-likelihood or approximate-maximum-likelihood tree
170sensitivity analysis was completed for the full 2,768-genome sample; shared systematic topology
171or branch-length error remains a limitation. Parametric null calibration and bootstrap analyses
172assess specified assumptions, rather than proving the phylogenetic model correct.
173Panel provenance and analysis scope
174The canonical stored panel predates deterministic paralogue tie-breaking. A corrected build
175sorts tied hits and output rows and reproduces byte-identically on repeated runs, but the
176canonical results have not all been migrated to that build. Refitting the authoritative headline
177table on the corrected panel changes +0.1848 to +0.1858 per standard deviation (0.035 standard
178errors), with no sign changes or p = 0.05 crossings among its 14 rows. This comparison bounds
179that measured impact; it does not make the original stored panel a byte-identical rebuild.
180Independent estimator replication begins from stored intermediates and does not independently
181validate phenotype curation, hit detection or structural annotation.
182Verification
183Verification used a static audit for nine previously encountered code-error classes, 26 unit
184tests, numerical traceability checks on the working Markdown manuscript and supporting
185documents, and an independently implemented PGLS estimator. The traceability check verifies that
186quoted numbers occur in a cited result table, but does not establish that a number supports its
187attached interpretation; it does not directly parse this Word document. The independent
188estimator shares no Newick parser, covariance construction, matrix factorisation or λ search
189with the original. Across 30 declared comparisons, its headline estimate is +0.184838 per
190standard deviation compared with +0.184837 in the original. These checks assess specified
191computational properties rather than independently validating all biological inputs.
192Results
193Exposed outward-facing composition is associated with reported minimum growth pH
194The composite of net acidic residue share at exposed extracytoplasmic positions gives +0.1848 pH
195units per standard deviation (2,768 genomes, P = 2 × 10⁻¹⁰) with genome-wide composition and
196source controlled (Fig. 1). The sign warrants stating explicitly, because it is easy to invert
197in prose: the coefficient is positive, so a higher net acidic share accompanies a higher
198minimum, that is, less acid tolerance. Acid-tolerant bacteria are depleted of acidic residues on
199their outward-facing extracytoplasmic surfaces, not enriched. At compartment level the contrast
200is direct: genomes with pH_min ≤ 3.5 average a secreted acidic share of 0.0999 against 0.1246
201for those at or above pH 6.

203Associations differ between membrane position classes
204Because the claim is about a compartment, the controls are the same measurement computed on
205other compartments of the same proteins, on the same genomes, in the same model (Fig. 1).
206Exposed cytoplasmic gives +0.0212 (P = 0.33) and transmembrane -0.0430 (P = 0.07); both are
207null. The soluble-surface reference set gives -0.0935 (P = 0.01), which is significant and
208opposite in sign. That is not a failure of specificity, but it does mean the pattern is a
209contrast between compartments rather than a periplasm-only effect, and we describe it as such.
210Entering both faces in a single model as sum and difference makes the extracytoplasmic excess an
211estimate in its own right: +0.0536 pH per percentage point (P = 3 × 10⁻⁶).
212Part of the association is independent of growth optimum and of histidine
213On the same 1,655 genomes with a recorded optimum, the headline mean-of-standardised-shares
214composite gives +0.2123 per standard deviation with composition and source controlled and
215+0.0904 with growth optimum also controlled. Figure 2 presents a separate pooled-share analysis
216on these genomes: adding growth optimum and exposed histidine together leaves +0.1120 per
217standard deviation (P = 4 × 10⁻³), or 39% of that pooled predictor’s own baseline coefficient.
218These coefficients come from different predictor definitions and should not be divided to
219estimate a retained percentage. The product of the pooled analysis’s separate retained shares is
2200.375, compared with a joint share of 0.387.
221 
222Exposed histidine is a separate association, not a proxy
223Exposed extracytoplasmic histidine share carries a larger coefficient than the acidic composite
224and in the opposite direction. Entered as sum and difference across the two compartments, with
225extracytoplasmic D, E and K held fixed, the shared component is -0.1317 pH per percentage point
226and the extracytoplasmic excess -0.0800 (P = 3 × 10⁻³) — so the association has both a side-
227specific and a shared part. That contrast was calibrated on the acidic result first, which fires
228at +0.0536, because a test is only a test once it has been shown capable of firing.
229The two measures correlate at r = −0.549, which makes one standing in for the other the obvious
230concern. Mutual adjustment excludes it: each retains roughly three quarters of its coefficient
231with the other held fixed, and the acidic measure's one-sidedness survives at 77% of its
232unadjusted value. Solvent access is doing demonstrable work — histidine at non-exposed positions
233in the same reference families predicts nothing, alone or jointly. A previously specified
234ordering of coefficients by side-chain pKa does not hold monotonically; what does organise them
235is charge class, which remains significant with histidine held fixed, while pKa orders the
236acidic pair and fails to order the basic residues.
237Detectable reference families are associated with reported minimum growth pH
238How many of the panel's reference families a genome has a detectable homologue of was initially
239held fixed as a nuisance control. Decomposed, it is two things: breadth survives at −0.036 pH
240per reference with assembly level, similarity to the reference proteins and genome size held
241fixed, whereas depth — positions per homologue — carries nothing once similarity is controlled.
242Of the accepted references, 22 have enough detection variance to regress on and 7 survive
243Benjamini–Hochberg at q < 0.10 (Fig. 3), the strongest being cytochrome bo(3) ubiquinol oxidase
244subunit 1 at -0.4286 pH.
245 
246Three limits are explicit. The intended negative control cannot be run: the soluble reference
247proteins are detected in over 99% of genomes and therefore fall below the variance floor, so
248none was tested, and a control that cannot run is a limitation rather than a pass. What replaces
249it is weaker and labelled so — the survivors' signs are mixed, five down and two up, which
250argues against a uniform directional artefact but does not exclude family-specific detection
251bias or establish function. Detection is a crude proxy for presence, so these are claims about
252detectable homologues of E. coli proteins. And breadth retains 47% of its coefficient with the
253survivors held fixed against a pre-set criterion of half, so the named references carry about
254half of the breadth effect rather than being it.
255The measure survives being made homogeneous, and the estimator reproduces
256The contribution of reference families varies between genomes, so holding reference breadth
257fixed does not guarantee that every genome’s composite measures identical positions. Sensitivity
258analyses use a six-reference common core and the 239 genomes carrying the complete reference
259set; the corresponding associations are +0.0362 and +0.0888 pH units per percentage point. These
260results support stability under those restrictions, without establishing that reference-level
261measurements are interchangeable.
262Discussion
263Three associations survive the controls designed to break them, and the compartment specificity
264is the part we would defend most firmly: the cytoplasmic and transmembrane controls are the
265identical measurement on other faces of the same proteins, and they are null. We think the
266limits below matter as much as the findings.
267Charge burden and buffering hypotheses
268The mechanism is contested by the one directly comparable experiment. Weeks and Sachs
269mutagenised every histidine, aspartate and glutamate in the extracytoplasmic domain of the H.
270pylori urea channel UreI and found three histidines and three carboxylates all required to
271activate transport at acidic pH, with those residues largely absent from the pH-independent
272Streptococcus salivarius homologue [3]. There, extracytoplasmic carboxylates are required for
273low-pH function; here they are depleted in acid-tolerant genomes. A later study adds a conserved
274cytoplasmic glutamate to the same channel's gating [13], and an engineering study aimed at
275gastric pH deliberately adds acidic residues on an explicit buffering rationale [14].
276We contrasted simplified charge-burden and buffering hypotheses using composition-derived
277titration summaries; these summaries do not identify a mechanism on their own. Writing Q(pH) for
278net surface charge and β(pH) = −dQ/dpH for buffer capacity, a charge-burden account predicts
279acid tolerance accompanying a smaller charge change between neutrality and the limit, while a
280buffering account predicts it accompanying greater capacity at the operating pH. At pH 3 and pH
2814, inside the acidophile range and near the carboxylate pKa values, the charge change carries a
282positive coefficient (+0.1399, P = 4 × 10⁻⁸; +0.0776, P = 1 × 10⁻³), so acid-tolerant genomes
283have less outward-facing charge to change, as burden predicts, while buffer capacity carries a
284positive coefficient, the opposite of what buffering predicts. Both quantities reverse sign
285between pH 4 and pH 6 (-0.2092), which is where histidine titrates and where its opposite-signed
286association appears inside the same quantity. We emphasise that this is not an electrostatics
287calculation — no structure, no solvent, no interaction between groups, and model pKa values
288throughout.
289A protein whose function is to respond to acid needs titratable groups on the face that senses
290it; a proteome that must merely continue working at low pH may do better with fewer. That
291reconciles UreI with the present result, and it makes a falsifiable prediction: a panel built
292from dedicated pH sensors should carry the opposite sign to a housekeeping panel. We attempted
293that test and it cannot be run on this sample. Dedicated extracytoplasmic sensor domains are
294detected in 1.9% of genomes, with all detections in a single phylum, so the contrast would be
295between lineages rather than between protein classes. The prediction is untested, not refuted.
296Reported minima and censoring sensitivity
297pH_min is meant to be the lowest pH at which an organism grows. What a species description
298usually records is the lowest pH at which growth was observed, bounded below by whatever the
299authors chose to test. If that choice tracks what they expected, the error is not noise but is
300systematically related to apparent acid tolerance and therefore to anything predicting it.
301The reported minima do carry a testing-grid signature: 74% sit on a whole pH unit and 93% on a
302whole or half unit, and the modal value, pH 6.0, accounts for 34% of the sample. A measured
303limit has no reason to prefer a round number; a testing grid has every reason to. Measured
304outcome reliability is high (ICC 0.965 from cross-source replicate readings, error SD 0.343 pH)
305and supports the standard result that error in the dependent variable inflates standard errors
306without biasing slopes — but that holds for classical error, independent of the predictor by
307construction. Censoring is not classical: it has a sign and a mechanism. Simulated at equal
308magnitude, classical noise moves the coefficient by 0.006 standard errors while the same
309magnitude aligned with the predictor moves it by 11.8.

312We evaluated specified observable censoring proxies. Erasing the headline slope requires
313censoring displacement to change by 0.185 pH units per predictor standard deviation (Fig. 4A).
314For the two proxies with effects in the threatening direction, the required mean displacements
315are at least 8.4 pH units, exceeding the roughly 6.0-unit headroom under an assumed lower bound
316of pH 0 (Fig. 4B). This bound applies only to those proxies and that assumed floor; pH 0 is not
317a universal physical lower limit. The proxies are weak and may capture other traits. Predictor-
318related censoring not represented by these proxies, or selection of organisms for description,
319remains unexcluded. Common-grid measurements or full-text classification of bound type would
320provide a stronger test.
321Reference transfer and envelope architecture
322The reference models are isolated chains and do not represent every oligomeric interface or
323species-specific structure. For alternative reference structures from the same Pfam family, per-
324genome measurements correlate weakly (median r = 0.126), although rescoring the same reference
325is reproducible. Topology-only annotation retains approximately 101% of the headline effect;
326solvent exposure concentrates signal per position but is not required for the aggregate
327association. The strongest interpretation is an outward-facing versus cytoplasmic contrast,
328rather than an exposure-specific causal effect.
329The association is present in both diderm and monoderm subsets. In a ten-phylum meta-regression,
330the monoderm increment is +0.1239 per standard deviation (SE 0.0788; one-sided P = 0.058).
331Envelope classification is coarse and partly confounded with phylogeny. These comparisons
332motivate consistent use of extracytoplasmic terminology and do not establish that outer-membrane
333architecture modifies the effect.
334What the association does not do
335It does not predict out of clade. Fitting on every other phylum, with the composite re-
336standardised on training genomes only, pooled out-of-sample RMSE is 0.9253 pH with the composite
337against 0.9248 pH without it — fractionally worse — and the cytoplasmic control, at 0.9238 pH,
338does no worse than either. The composite lowers error in three of four folds, and the pooled
339figure is decided by the one it fails, which is the phylum holding most of the acidophiles and
340whose removal leaves the training set with little of the acid-tolerant range the effect is
341confined to; fold-level gain tracks the tail retained in training at r = +0.835 across four
342folds, a direction predicted in advance and resting on four points. Both readings stand: the
343association survives every within-sample test reported here and buys no out-of-sample accuracy
344across clades. These results support an in-sample conditional association and do not demonstrate
345generalisable prediction or a causal mechanism. Reference selection was additionally performed
346once on all genomes, so even these figures are optimistic by an unmeasured amount.
347The effects are small in variance terms, well under 1% of pH_min variance beyond the growth
348optimum, and the relationship is concentrated in the acid-tolerant tail rather than running
349uniformly across the range. Nothing here is interventional. In archaea the pattern is consistent
350but marginal, and within halophilic archaea specificity fails outright.
351A second environmental variable acts on a different compartment
352Because compartment-resolved isoelectric point shifts with marine-versus-freshwater habitat in
353the same ordering reported here [7], a habitat variable could in principle act through the
354extracytoplasmic face and mimic the result. Measured salinity tolerance, available for 669
355genomes of this sample, does not predict pH_min (P = 0.36) and removes 1% of the
356extracytoplasmic coefficient when held fixed. More informative, the two variables have separable
357compartment profiles: salinity gives secreted > cytoplasmic > membrane, reproducing the
358published ordering, while pH_min gives secreted > membrane > cytoplasmic. Salinity moves the
359secreted and cytoplasmic shares together, as a whole-cell osmotic account predicts; pH moves the
360secreted share and leaves the cytoplasmic one untouched. The method does not impose one ordering
361on every variable given to it, which is a stronger defence of the controls than salinity being
362inert — it is not inert, and predicts secreted composition more strongly than pH does. Within
363the salinity-measured subsample the transmembrane control does fire, and that signal is confined
364to the salt-tolerant half, consistent with halotolerant membranes being compositionally
365distinct: a second environmental variable perturbing a different compartment from the one
366claimed here.
367Conclusions
368Extracytoplasmic-facing charge composition in the reference panel is associated with reported
369minimum growth pH after adjustment for growth optimum and histidine. The direction is lower net
370acidic composition in acid-tolerant organisms. Composition-derived titration summaries are
371consistent with a charge-burden hypothesis, but do not establish it over alternative mechanisms.
372The outcome is shaped by growth-testing conventions, the phylogeny and structural annotation
373impose additional assumptions, and the composite provides no out-of-clade predictive gain. An
374experimental charge-reversal protocol has been specified but not performed; causal
375interpretation awaits experimental evidence.
