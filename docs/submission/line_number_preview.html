11Abstract
12How acidic an environment a bacterium will grow in is only partly explained by where it grows
13best, and the genomic basis of the lower limit itself is not established. We asked which genomic
14features predict the minimum growth pH (pH_min) beyond what the growth optimum already explains,
15using 2,768 bacterial genomes with a curated minimum and a 32-protein panel of conserved inner-
16membrane reference proteins with per-residue structural confidence filtering. Analyses are
17phylogenetic generalised least squares with Pagel's λ, controlling genome-wide amino acid
18composition and data source throughout. Composition at solvent-exposed periplasmic positions
19predicts the limit: a composite of net acidic residue share gives +0.1848 pH units per standard
20deviation (P = 2 × 10⁻10), and the direction is depletion, acid-tolerant bacteria carrying fewer
21exposed periplasmic acidic residues. Matched cytoplasmic (+0.0212, P = 0.33) and transmembrane
22(-0.0430, P = 0.07) controls computed on the same proteins are null, and the periplasmic-minus-
23cytoplasmic difference, estimated directly so that it carries a standard error, is +0.0536 pH
24per percentage point (P = 3 × 10⁻6). The association retains 39% of its size with growth optimum
25and exposed histidine controlled together (+0.1120 per SD, P = 4 × 10⁻3). Exposed histidine
26carries a separate, larger and oppositely signed association that is not a proxy for the first.
27The presence of particular membrane complexes predicts the limit independently of composition,
28with 7 of 22 testable reference families surviving Benjamini–Hochberg correction at q < 0.10.
29The effects are small in variance terms, the work is correlational, and the reported minima
30largely sit on an experimental testing grid rather than on a demonstrated biological boundary;
31we bound rather than dismiss what that could do, and show that no observable censoring pattern
32could account for the association. The composite does not improve out-of-sample prediction in
33clades the model has not seen, which we report in full.

52Impact Statement
53Compositional signatures of extremophily are well established for temperature and salinity but
54not for pH, which a standing review describes as considerably less discernible. The mechanisms
55of bacterial acid resistance that are understood in detail are inducible responses —
56decarboxylase systems, urea import, dedicated pH sensors — and they explain survival of acid
57shock better than they explain the pH at which steady growth stops. This study asks what sets
58that limit, and finds a signal in the composition of the one compartment the hypothesis names in
59advance: the outward-facing periplasmic surface, which unlike the cytoplasm is not held near
60neutrality. Because the prediction is compartment-specific, it comes with controls that are the
61same measurement on other faces of the same proteins, and those controls are null. The work also
62shows what such an analysis cannot deliver: the association does not transfer as a predictor to
63clades the model has not seen, and the outcome variable is bounded by how species descriptions
64are written rather than by biology. We quantify that bound instead of noting it. For readers
65building genome-based trait predictors, the reported limits may be as useful as the association.

67Introduction
68Bacteria differ enormously in how acidic an environment they will grow in. Some isolates of
69Acidithiobacillus and Picrophilus grow below pH 1; most described species will not grow below pH
705. What sets that lower bound is not the same question as where an organism grows best, and the
71two are routinely conflated because the optimum is far more often reported. A species whose
72optimum is pH 7 may have a limit at pH 4 or at pH 6, and the difference between those organisms
73is the quantity of interest here.
74The mechanisms of acid resistance that are understood in detail are inducible and physiological.
75Enteric bacteria surviving gastric transit deploy amino-acid decarboxylase systems that consume
76cytoplasmic protons, regulated by sensors that detect acidification directly: the EvgS/EvgA two-
77component system, in which a single histidine in the periplasmic domain is required for acid
78induction [1], and the regulator AdiY, whose pH response runs through the protonation of two
79histidines [2]. Helicobacter pylori additionally imports urea through the pH-gated channel UreI
80to feed intrabacterial urease [3]. Experimental evolution for acid resistance in Escherichia
81coli converges on the same signalling machinery, with evgS mutated in every selected population
82[4]. These are responses: machinery switched on when the environment turns acidic, and they
83explain survival of acid shock better than they explain the pH at which steady growth becomes
84impossible.
85A limit might instead be set by what the cell's proteins can tolerate, and if so the relevant
86compartment is identifiable in advance. A Gram-negative bacterium holds its cytoplasm near
87neutrality even in strong acid, so cytoplasmic proteins never experience the external pH. The
88periplasm has no such protection: it equilibrates far closer to the medium, and proteins with
89surfaces facing it operate at whatever pH the organism is growing in. Any compositional
90signature of acid adaptation should therefore appear on outward-facing surfaces and not on
91cytoplasmic ones — which makes the hypothesis testable against a control that is the same
92measurement on a different face of the same protein. Consistent with the compartment mattering,
93periplasmic redox proteins of acidophiles have systematically more positive redox potentials
94than neutrophilic homologues [5].
95Compositional signatures of other extremes are established; for pH they are not. Thermophily is
96visible in the IVYWREL fraction and halophily in a pronounced excess of surface acidic residues,
97while a review of archaeal extremophile protein adaptation reports that adaptations toward pH
98are considerably less discernible [6]. That is a documented absence rather than an assumption,
99and it is the gap addressed here.
100Two recent results make the question tractable. Cabello-Yeves et al. showed that predicted
101isoelectric point shifts with habitat between marine and freshwater prokaryotes, with the
102difference sharper in secreted than cytoplasmic than membrane proteins and concentrated on
103protein surfaces [7], establishing that compartment-resolved composition carries environmental
104signal. Independently, Barnum et al. showed that growth pH can be predicted from amino acid
105composition computed per predicted subcellular localization, reporting the extracellular-minus-
106intracellular glutamate difference as the strongest residue-level correlate [8]. Together these
107say that the compartment-resolved signal exists and that pH is among the variables it carries.
108We ask which genomic features predict pH_min beyond the growth optimum, and whether any such
109signal is specific to the compartment the hypothesis names. Three associations survive the
110controls, and the limits that bound them are reported as fully as the associations themselves.
111Methods
112Phenotype data
113The analysis sample is 2,768 bacterial genomes carrying a curated minimum growth pH and a
114position in the reference phylogeny. Minima were compiled from four source tiers, recorded per
115record: hand-curated species descriptions read from primary sources, rule-based extraction from
116species-description abstracts, ThermoBase, and AciDB/BacDive [9]. Source is entered as a set of
117indicator covariates in every model, so a between-source level difference cannot by itself
118produce an association.
119The extraction tier dominates the sample and its fidelity was measured rather than assumed.
120Against hand-curated records it is exact for 98.8% of minima. Because that validation set was
121acidophile-enriched, 25 further records were read by hand from the pH band carrying most of the
122data; 3 of 25 were wrong, all by one mechanism, in which a sentence reporting only an optimum
123has that optimum recorded as the growth range. A detector for that specific failure scores 8 of
1248 against fifteen hand-read statements with no false positives and flags 298 of 2,536 extracted
125records; excluding exactly those records moves the headline estimate by 0.13 standard errors.
126Structural panel and composite
127The predictor is built on a panel of conserved inner-membrane proteins of Escherichia coli with
128experimentally determined or high-confidence predicted structures. Each reference position was
129assigned to a class — exposed periplasmic, exposed cytoplasmic, transmembrane or buried — from
130UniProt topology together with relative solvent accessibility of the isolated model, computed by
131the Shrake–Rupley algorithm and normalised to the maxima of Tien et al. [10], with exposure
132defined as RSA ≥ 0.25. A position enters only if its per-residue pLDDT is ≥ 70, since the
133solvent accessibility of a poorly predicted residue is not interpretable. For each genome, the
134best-aligning hit of the reference's Pfam family was tallied by the class of the reference
135position it aligned to, counting D, E, K, R and H separately.
136The composite is the mean across references of the per-reference net acidic share (D + E − K −
137R) divided by scorable positions, each reference standardised across genomes before averaging,
138with a per-reference position floor and a minimum of three contributing references. The panel
139used throughout is a 32-reference rule-selected set; a 7-reference hand-picked panel preceded it
140and is not the same measurement, so per-unit coefficients from the two are never compared. Panel
141construction is deterministic and verified to rebuild byte-identically.
142Statistical model
143All estimates are phylogenetic generalised least squares on a Brownian covariance matrix derived
144from the reference marker-gene tree, with Pagel's λ [11] estimated by maximum likelihood. Every
145model carries an intercept, a genome-wide amino acid composition control and the source
146indicators. Coefficients are reported per standard deviation of the predictor unless a per-unit
147figure is explicitly labelled, because composites averaging different numbers of references have
148different spreads. Where a model adds the growth optimum, the comparison is made on the single
149sample for which the optimum is known, never against the full-sample figure, so that a change of
150model is not confounded with a change of sample. Multiple testing across reference families is
151controlled by the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure [12].
152Compartment contrasts are estimated by entering both faces in one model as their sum and
153difference. This spans the same column space as entering the two levels and so changes no fitted
154value, but it makes the periplasmic excess an estimate carrying its own standard error, which a
155difference taken between two separately fitted coefficients does not.
156Verification
157Four checks were required to pass before any figure was quoted. A static audit searches the
158analysis code for nine previously made error classes. A unit-test suite covers the library,
159including mutation-checked guards on the per-standard-deviation/per-unit identity. A
160traceability check extracts every signed coefficient from the manuscript and supporting
161documents and requires it to be present in the stored result file that the same claim cites.
162Finally, the estimator was reimplemented independently, sharing no Newick parser, no covariance
163construction, no matrix factorisation and no λ search with the original: across 30 declared
164comparisons the headline reproduces at +0.184838 per SD against +0.184837.
165Results
166Exposed periplasmic acidic composition predicts the lower growth limit
167The composite of net acidic residue share at exposed periplasmic positions gives +0.1848 pH
168units per standard deviation (2,768 genomes, P = 2 × 10⁻10) with genome-wide composition and
169source controlled (Fig. 1). The sign warrants stating explicitly, because it is easy to invert
170in prose: the coefficient is positive, so a higher net acidic share accompanies a higher
171minimum, that is, less acid tolerance. Acid-tolerant bacteria are depleted of acidic residues on
172their outward-facing periplasmic surfaces, not enriched. At compartment level the contrast is
173direct: genomes with pH_min ≤ 3.5 average a secreted acidic share of 0.0999 against 0.1246 for
174those at or above pH 6.
175The association is confined to the periplasmic-facing surface
176Because the claim is about a compartment, the controls are the same measurement computed on
177other compartments of the same proteins, on the same genomes, in the same model (Fig. 1).
178Exposed cytoplasmic gives +0.0212 (P = 0.33) and transmembrane -0.0430 (P = 0.07); both are
179null. The soluble-surface reference set gives -0.0935 (P = 0.01), which is significant and
180opposite in sign. That is not a failure of specificity, but it does mean the pattern is a
181contrast between compartments rather than a periplasm-only effect, and we describe it as such.
182Entering both faces in a single model as sum and difference makes the periplasmic excess an
183estimate in its own right: +0.0536 pH per percentage point (P = 3 × 10⁻6).
184Part of the association is independent of growth optimum and of histidine
185On the genomes whose optimum is known, so that the drop is a change of model alone, the
186composition-only coefficient of +0.2123 per SD falls to +0.0904 with the optimum controlled
187(Fig. 2). The more demanding test holds the optimum and exposed histidine fixed together, since
188histidine carries its own association. The coefficient is +0.1120 per SD (P = 4 × 10⁻3),
189retaining 39% of the composition-only figure on that sample. The two controls prove nearly
190independent: the product of their separate shares is 0.375 against a joint share of 0.387.
191Exposed histidine is a separate association, not a proxy
192Exposed periplasmic histidine share carries a larger coefficient than the acidic composite and
193in the opposite direction. Entered as sum and difference across the two compartments, with
194periplasmic D, E and K held fixed, the shared component is -0.1317 pH per percentage point and
195the periplasmic excess -0.0800 (P = 3 × 10⁻3) — so the association has both a side-specific and
196a shared part. That contrast was calibrated on the acidic result first, which fires at +0.0536,
197because a test is only a test once it has been shown capable of firing.
198The two measures correlate at r = −0.549, which makes one standing in for the other the obvious
199concern. Mutual adjustment excludes it: each retains roughly three quarters of its coefficient
200with the other held fixed, and the acidic measure's one-sidedness survives at 77% of its
201unadjusted value. Solvent access is doing demonstrable work — histidine at non-exposed positions
202in the same reference families predicts nothing, alone or jointly. A pre-registered ordering of
203coefficients by side-chain pKa does not hold monotonically; what does organise them is charge
204class, which remains significant with histidine held fixed, while pKa orders the acidic pair and
205fails to order the basic residues.
206Detectable presence of particular membrane complexes predicts the limit
207How many of the panel's reference families a genome has a detectable homologue of was initially
208held fixed as a nuisance control. Decomposed, it is two things: breadth survives at −0.036 pH
209per reference with assembly level, similarity to the reference proteins and genome size held
210fixed, whereas depth — positions per homologue — carries nothing once similarity is controlled.
211Of the accepted references, 22 have enough detection variance to regress on and 7 survive
212Benjamini–Hochberg at q < 0.10 (Fig. 3), the strongest being cytochrome bo(3) ubiquinol oxidase
213subunit 1 at -0.4286 pH.
214Three limits are explicit. The intended negative control cannot be run: the soluble reference
215proteins are detected in over 99% of genomes and therefore fall below the variance floor, so
216none was tested, and a control that cannot run is a limitation rather than a pass. What replaces
217it is weaker and labelled so — the survivors' signs are mixed, five down and two up, which
218excludes a shared sequencing or annotation artefact because such an account predicts one
219direction for every reference, and establishes nothing about function. Detection is a crude
220proxy for presence, so these are claims about detectable homologues of E. coli proteins. And
221breadth retains 47% of its coefficient with the survivors held fixed against a pre-set criterion
222of half, so the named references carry about half of the breadth effect rather than being it.
223The measure survives being made homogeneous, and the estimator reproduces
224The composite pools numerator and denominator over whichever references a genome has a
225detectable homologue of, so two genomes with identical surface chemistry can receive different
226shares. This is heterogeneity in what the measure measures, which holding breadth fixed in a
227regression does not remove. Two routes that fail in different places were run: a six-reference
228common core identical across genomes, and the 239 genomes carrying every reference. Neither
229failed, at +0.0362 and +0.0888 pH per percentage point respectively.
230Discussion
231Three associations survive the controls designed to break them, and the compartment specificity
232is the part we would defend most firmly: the cytoplasmic and transmembrane controls are the
233identical measurement on other faces of the same proteins, and they are null. We think the
234limits below matter as much as the findings.
235Mechanism: charge burden rather than buffering
236The mechanism is contested by the one directly comparable experiment. Weeks and Sachs
237mutagenised every histidine, aspartate and glutamate in the periplasmic domain of the H. pylori
238urea channel UreI and found three histidines and three carboxylates all required to activate
239transport at acidic pH, with those residues largely absent from the pH-independent Streptococcus
240salivarius homologue [3]. There, periplasmic carboxylates are required for low-pH function; here
241they are depleted in acid-tolerant genomes. A later study adds a conserved cytoplasmic glutamate
242to the same channel's gating [13], and an engineering study aimed at gastric pH deliberately
243adds acidic residues on an explicit buffering rationale [14].
244The two accounts are separable from composition alone, because they require opposite signs of
245the same quantity. Writing Q(pH) for net surface charge and β(pH) = −dQ/dpH for buffer capacity,
246a charge-burden account predicts acid tolerance accompanying a smaller charge change between
247neutrality and the limit, while a buffering account predicts it accompanying greater capacity at
248the operating pH. At pH 3 and pH 4, inside the acidophile range and near the carboxylate pKa
249values, the charge change carries a positive coefficient (+0.1399, P = 4 × 10⁻8; +0.0776, P = 1
250× 10⁻3), so acid-tolerant genomes have less outward-facing charge to change, as burden predicts,
251while buffer capacity carries a positive coefficient, the opposite of what buffering predicts.
252Both quantities reverse sign between pH 4 and pH 6 (-0.2092), which is where histidine titrates
253and where its opposite-signed association appears inside the same quantity. We emphasise that
254this is not an electrostatics calculation — no structure, no solvent, no interaction between
255groups, and model pKa values throughout.
256A protein whose function is to respond to acid needs titratable groups on the face that senses
257it; a proteome that must merely continue working at low pH may do better with fewer. That
258reconciles UreI with the present result, and it makes a falsifiable prediction: a panel built
259from dedicated pH sensors should carry the opposite sign to a housekeeping panel. We attempted
260that test and it cannot be run on this sample. Dedicated periplasmic sensor domains are detected
261in 1.9% of genomes, with all detections in a single phylum, so the contrast would be between
262lineages rather than between protein classes. The prediction is untested, not refuted.
263The outcome variable is the principal limitation, and it is bounded
264pH_min is meant to be the lowest pH at which an organism grows. What a species description
265usually records is the lowest pH at which growth was observed, bounded below by whatever the
266authors chose to test. If that choice tracks what they expected, the error is not noise but is
267systematically related to apparent acid tolerance and therefore to anything predicting it.
268The reported minima do carry a testing-grid signature: 74% sit on a whole pH unit and 93% on a
269whole or half unit, and the modal value, pH 6.0, accounts for 34% of the sample. A measured
270limit has no reason to prefer a round number; a testing grid has every reason to. Measured
271outcome reliability is high (ICC 0.965 from cross-source replicate readings, error SD 0.343 pH)
272and supports the standard result that error in the dependent variable inflates standard errors
273without biasing slopes — but that holds for classical error, independent of the predictor by
274construction. Censoring is not classical: it has a sign and a mechanism. Simulated at equal
275magnitude, classical noise moves the coefficient by 0.006 standard errors while the same
276magnitude aligned with the predictor moves it by 11.8.
277We therefore bounded it. To drive the coefficient to zero, the censoring displacement would have
278to deepen by 0.185 pH for every standard deviation of surface composition. Routed through
279observable proxies for being censored, that requirement is not merely implausible but physically
280impossible: the proxies pointing in the threatening direction require mean displacements of 8.4
281pH or more against 6.0 pH of available headroom, which would place the flagged organisms' true
282minima below pH 0 (Fig. 4). This closes the objection in the form a proxy can express it. It
283does not close the objection: the proxies are weak and each proxies other things, and selection
284acting on which organisms get described at all would appear in none of them. Settling it
285requires bound type read from full texts at scale, or growth limits measured on a common pH
286grid.
287What the association does not do
288It does not predict out of clade. Fitting on every other phylum, with the composite re-
289standardised on training genomes only, pooled out-of-sample RMSE is 0.9253 pH with the composite
290against 0.9248 pH without it — fractionally worse — and the cytoplasmic control, at 0.9238 pH,
291does no worse than either. The composite lowers error in three of four folds, and the pooled
292figure is decided by the one it fails, which is the phylum holding most of the acidophiles and
293whose removal leaves the training set with little of the acid-tolerant range the effect is
294confined to; fold-level gain tracks the tail retained in training at r = +0.835 across four
295folds, a direction predicted in advance and resting on four points. Both readings stand: the
296association survives every within-sample test reported here and buys no out-of-sample accuracy
297across clades. For a claim about mechanism that is survivable; for a claim about prediction it
298would be fatal, and we make none. Reference selection was additionally performed once on all
299genomes, so even these figures are optimistic by an unmeasured amount.
300The effects are small in variance terms, well under 1% of pH_min variance beyond the growth
301optimum, and the relationship is concentrated in the acid-tolerant tail rather than running
302uniformly across the range. Nothing here is interventional. In archaea the pattern is consistent
303but marginal, and within halophilic archaea specificity fails outright.
304A second environmental variable acts on a different compartment
305Because compartment-resolved isoelectric point shifts with marine-versus-freshwater habitat in
306the same ordering reported here [7], a habitat variable could in principle act through the
307periplasmic face and mimic the result. Measured salinity tolerance, available for 669 genomes of
308this sample, does not predict pH_min (P = 0.36) and removes 1% of the periplasmic coefficient
309when held fixed. More informative, the two variables have separable compartment profiles:
310salinity gives secreted > cytoplasmic > membrane, reproducing the published ordering, while
311pH_min gives secreted > membrane > cytoplasmic. Salinity moves the secreted and cytoplasmic
312shares together, as a whole-cell osmotic account predicts; pH moves the secreted share and
313leaves the cytoplasmic one untouched. The method does not impose one ordering on every variable
314given to it, which is a stronger defence of the controls than salinity being inert — it is not
315inert, and predicts secreted composition more strongly than pH does. Within the salinity-
316measured subsample the transmembrane control does fire, and that signal is confined to the salt-
317tolerant half, consistent with halotolerant membranes being compositionally distinct: a second
318environmental variable perturbing a different compartment from the one claimed here.
319Conclusion
320Composition at the outward-facing periplasmic surface is associated with the lower pH limit of
321bacterial growth beyond what growth optimum explains, specifically to that compartment, and
322separably from a larger histidine association and from gene content. The direction is depletion
323of acidic residues, and a charge-burden rather than buffering account is the one the titration
324behaviour supports, against the single most directly comparable experiment in the literature.
325The limits are as firm as the findings: no out-of-clade predictive gain, an outcome variable
326shaped by how species descriptions are written, and no interventional evidence. A pre-registered
327charge-reversal experiment is designed; the discrimination between burden and buffering should
328be settled on a single chassis protein before it is run.
